![Stop Making Live Action Slop!](/images/posts/stop-making-live-action-slop/image-1.jpg)

Another year, another beloved animated thing dragged kicking and screaming into a live action remake nobody asked for.

Here's the pattern at this point: take something animated and beloved, strip out everything that made it work, slap real actors and "grounded" cinematography on it, and call it a _**reimagining**_. It is not.

It's a faded photocopy with a bigger budget, made by people who clearly think the only thing wrong with the original was that it wasn't expensive enough. And the maddening part is we keep watching, because nostalgia is a hell of a drug and these studios know it better than anyone. They're not making these for people who've never seen the original. They're making them for the people who already love it, betting we'll show up out of loyalty even when we know exactly what we're walking into. Genius business model, honestly. Hand them the same meal they already loved, just slightly burnt this time, and watch them pay full price anyway.

* * *

### Aladdin

![Stop Making Live Action Slop!](/images/posts/stop-making-live-action-slop/image-2.jpg)

Let's start with this one, since it's basically the blueprint for how shameless this whole thing has gotten. Critics were lukewarm on it at best, the reception was mixed, and it still crossed a billion dollars worldwide. **A billion!**

That's the whole game laid bare right there: it doesn't need to be good, it just needs to exist and wear a familiar face. Will Smith's Genie got memed into oblivion before the movie even came out and Disney still walked away with one of the biggest paydays of their entire live action slate. So why would they stop? Why would any studio stop doing the thing that prints money regardless of whether the final product is actually worth your two hours?

**They're not in the business of art, they're in the business of nostalgia laundering, and business is booming.**

### The Lion King

![Stop Making Live Action Slop!](/images/posts/stop-making-live-action-slop/image-3.jpg)

I will go to my grave saying this one is an abomination. Critics called it a "soulless chimera" and even compared it to a tech demo, said it played like the world's longest, least convincing deepfake. And they were right.

The whole appeal of the original was how expressive it was, how much character and rage and grief you could pack into a hand drawn face. Scar wasn't just evil, he was deliciously, theatrically evil, all curled lips and venomous wit. In the remake he's just a lion that sounds like Chiwetel Ejiofor, standing there with the same blank photorealistic expression he uses for literally every other emotion. Truly groundbreaking work, having a real lion stare at the camera with the same face whether he's plotting a coup or asking for the time. You cannot animate real lion faces into delivering Shakespearean villainy and act like that's a minor technical hiccup instead of the entire point of why the original worked.

### The Mulan Remake

![Stop Making Live Action Slop!](/images/posts/stop-making-live-action-slop/image-4.jpg)

**Mulan** is where I stop being annoyed and start being genuinely offended.

They cut the songs. Okay. fine, I can live with that if the story still hits. They cut Mushu, alright, I get the instinct even if I think they lost something. But then they took Mulan's entire arc, _**the whole point of her character,**_ and shat on it.

The original story is about a girl who has no special powers, no destiny, nothing handed to her, who succeeds through grit and cleverness despite everyone telling her she doesn't belong. That's the whole theme.

In the remake they gave her **Qi**, basically supernatural warrior powers she's had since childhood, which means she was never actually the underdog. _She was just hiding a cheat code the whole time._

Bold move, taking the one Disney princess who succeeded purely on merit and quietly handing her a superpower instead. Whoever sat in that room and approved it deserves to explain themselves on camera. It's not even a bold creative swing. It's a fundamental misunderstanding of why people loved the original in the first place.

### Netflix's Avatar The Last Airbender

![Stop Making Live Action Slop!](/images/posts/stop-making-live-action-slop/image-5.jpg)

And now we've got Netflix's **Avatar: The Last Airbender**, and I'm exhausted before I've even finished typing this sentence. They stripped the humor that made the original so beloved and replaced it with grim, joyless line readings.

Katara, who was fierce and funny and full of motherly steel in the cartoon, got flattened into what one reviewer accurately called a sad sack. And the dialogue. My god, the dialogue. Characters don't talk to each other, they just deliver information, full scenes that exist purely to explain plot points the animated series trusted its audience to pick up through actual storytelling. Riveting stuff, really, two people standing in a room reading the Wikipedia summary of the plot out loud to each other.

That's the actual crime here. These remakes assume the audience needs everything spelled out, every emotional beat underlined twice, every plot point explained out loud, because apparently we can't be trusted to pay attention to our own television anymore.

**Animation never had to dumb itself down like this, and the fact that live action adaptations consistently do says everything about how little these studios actually trust their own audience.**

* * *

### Final Thoughts

Look, I'm not saying every adaptation in history has been garbage. I'm saying the live action well has officially run dry, and instead of admitting that, these studios keep dipping the same empty bucket back into it and acting surprised when nothing comes up. There are original stories, original animated shows, original ideas sitting untouched while executives green light remake number forty seven of something that was already perfect the first time. Leave animation alone. Let it be its own medium instead of treating it like a rough draft for the "real" version.
