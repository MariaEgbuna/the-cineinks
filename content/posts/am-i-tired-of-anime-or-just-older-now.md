People keep asking if I've fallen out of love with anime, and the answer is no. This is not a "quitting anime" post. I can throw on *Attack on Titan* and lose my whole evening to it like it's the first time, and I'm counting down the days for *Haikyuu* and *Vinland Saga* updates like a kid waiting for Christmas.

But lately I'll sit down to start something new, scroll my watchlist for twenty minutes, and close the app. And when I do commit and the show turns out average, I don't shrug it off like I used to. I feel drained, like I threw away hours I'm never getting back.

## Why does there feel like too much anime?

I think this comes down to two shifts feeding into each other. The first is volume. Anime isn't a niche subculture you had to dig for on sketchy forums anymore. It's a global, multi-billion-dollar assembly line. Netflix alone logged 4.4 billion anime viewing hours in the first half of 2025, and that's one platform, not counting Crunchyroll, HIDIVE, or Hulu.

A single season can bring sequels or adaptations for *Jujutsu Kaisen*, *Frieren*, *Vigilantes*, *Hell's Paradise*, *Oshi no Ko*, *Golden Kamuy*, a new *Fate* project, and the finale of *Fire Force* in the same three months. Add the hundreds of smaller slice-of-life shows and light novel adaptations, and it stops feeling like a hobby and starts feeling like static.

With that much to filter, picking wrong feels expensive. You give a seasonal show six episodes, it goes nowhere, and that time is gone. So I don't pick anything. It's decision paralysis dressed up as fatigue.

## What did Solo Leveling show me?

![Anime Fatigue: Am I Tired of Anime, or Just Older Now?](/images/posts/am-i-tired-of-anime-or-just-older-now/image-2.jpg)

*Solo Leveling* is the textbook case. The hype was deafening, and I expected to be floored. What I got was flashy power-up sequences stapled onto a story I've seen a hundred times: a weak, undervalued guy gets a secret power, becomes the strongest hunter alive, and everyone who doubted him regrets it.

The animation from A-1 Pictures is gorgeous, and I'm not taking that away from them. But a great coat of paint on a plot I can chart from episode two doesn't hold my attention anymore.

## Why are isekai titles so long?

![Anime Fatigue: Am I Tired of Anime, or Just Older Now?](/images/posts/am-i-tired-of-anime-or-just-older-now/image-3.jpg)

Isekai saturation isn't helping. The genre is so crowded that studios now use the whole plot summary as the title. Have you scrolled past ***Reborn as a Vending Machine, I Now Wander the Dungeon***? I'm serious. A man dies, gets reincarnated as a sentient vending machine in a fantasy dungeon, and the show ran three seasons from 2023 to 2026 on how bizarre it was.

It's not just the comedies. ***The Strongest Tank's Labyrinth Raids: A Tank with a Rare 9999 Resistance Skill Got Kicked from the Hero's Party*** is a full sentence, not a title. ***Even Given the Worthless [Appraiser] Class, I'm Actually the Strongest*** spoils its own twist before you press play.

Apparently the trend started because light novel readers stopped reading back-cover descriptions, so the titles had to do the work. Now it's a conveyor belt of "I Got Reincarnated As X And Now I'm Overpowered In This Specific Way," with the variables swapped every season. The titles keep getting longer because the ideas keep getting thinner.

## Is it just that my time is harder to gamble now?

The second half, and the one I'm less comfortable admitting, is that I've gotten more protective of my time as I've gotten older. I haven't aged out of anime in a sad way. I just know what I like and have no patience left for filler.

When I was younger, a 6/10 show was fine. I had time to burn after school or work, and I'd sit through a slow ten-episode intro because a stranger online promised it "gets really good in season two." Now, when I clear space to watch something, I need it to deliver. An average show doesn't feel middle-of-the-road anymore. It feels like a net loss. I could have rewatched a comfort show I *know* is a masterpiece, or taken a nap that would have been worth more.

That's probably just the grumpy old lady in me talking.

## So am I tired of anime?

No. I'm tired of the constant noise, and I've lost my patience for shows that don't respect my time. Together those can look like burnout from the outside, but it's a different thing.

The fix is being a lot more selective, leaning on a small circle of trusted recommendations instead of whatever is trending, and giving myself full permission to drop a show the second it stops working for me.
