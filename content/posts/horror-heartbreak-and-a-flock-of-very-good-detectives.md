Five days, nine movies, and three of them didn't survive past the one-hour mark.

It started with a John Wick rewatch, and somewhere around the credits I decided I wanted horror for the rest of the week. That turned into an accidental Evil Dead deep dive, then bent into whatever new horror release was in my queue: Leviticus, Backrooms, and Hokum. By the end I'd watched a bunch of talking sheep solve a murder and actually got a little emotional about it.

Here's how it played out, in the order I watched it.

---

## John Wick: Chapter 3 - Parabellum (2019)

![John Wick: Chapter 3 - Parabellum (2019)](/images/posts/horror-heartbreak-and-a-flock-of-very-good-detectives/image-2.jpg)

This is the rewatch that started it all. Everything's turned up from the first two films: more assassins, more lore, more absurd fight choreography, and more of Keanu Reeves getting thrown through glass than should be physically survivable. It's not trying to be deep, and it doesn't need to be, because sometimes you just want to watch a man break someone's neck with a book. **8.3/10.** A fun watch from start to finish.

## The Evil Dead franchise: Evil Dead (1981), Evil Dead II, Army of Darkness and Evil Dead (2013)

![The Evil Dead franchise](/images/posts/horror-heartbreak-and-a-flock-of-very-good-detectives/image-3.jpg)

I went straight into the original, **The Evil Dead**, the low budget cabin in the woods film that basically wrote the rulebook every possession horror movie since has been copying. I fell asleep twice trying to get through it, but it was okay for most of the runtime, right up until the ending, where the gross practical effects mixed with animation threw me off completely. The acting and effects both got so over the top that it came in at a **6.0/10**: decent, but not one I'd rush back to.

I tried to keep the momentum going and it fell apart. **Evil Dead II** went full comedy, which isn't what I signed up for, so it got dropped. **Army of Darkness** didn't even make it that far.

What worked was the 2013 remake, **Evil Dead**, which I watched the same day Army of Darkness got cut loose. It was loud and chaotic, with all the blood and gore remakes tend to reach for. It's not really my lane of horror, but it held my attention enough for a **7.0/10**. I'm not done with the franchise: Evil Dead Rise (2023) and the new Evil Dead Burn (2026) are next. The two I dropped will stay dropped.

## Leviticus (2026)

![Leviticus](/images/posts/horror-heartbreak-and-a-flock-of-very-good-detectives/image-4.jpg)

The queer horror gut punch of the week. It follows two teenage boys in a remote Christian community whose feelings for each other start manifesting as a violent supernatural entity, and it doesn't hide that it's a metaphor for conversion therapy and religious repression. I really loved this movie. My only complaint was the runtime. It felt too short for how attached I'd gotten to these two, my poor babies, and I closed it wishing for an extra twenty minutes. **8.5/10**, appreciation and mild heartbreak included.

## Backrooms (2026)

![Backrooms](/images/posts/horror-heartbreak-and-a-flock-of-very-good-detectives/image-5.jpg)

All atmosphere, no substance. Kane Parsons, the creator behind the viral Backrooms YouTube series, made his feature directorial debut here, and the liminal space visuals are properly unsettling on their own. My problem is everything wrapped around them. A therapist chasing a missing patient into an alternate dimension had strong potential, but the story kept reaching for a meaning it never built up to. Pretentious is the word, and **6.3/10** feels right. I know some people loved it and will tell me I just don't get it. That's fine, let's agree to disagree.

## Hokum (2026)

![Hokum](/images/posts/horror-heartbreak-and-a-flock-of-very-good-detectives/image-6.jpg)

The one that just didn't work, and the last horror movie of the week. Damian McCarthy, the director of Oddity, brings the same slow burn atmospheric style to a story about a novelist confronting a witch legend while scattering his parents' ashes at a remote Irish inn. Cheap scares and a whole lot of meh. I know it's supposed to be sitting with grief underneath the ghost story trappings, but I couldn't bring myself to care. I dropped it around the one hour mark.

## The Sheep Detectives (2026)

![The Sheep Detectives](/images/posts/horror-heartbreak-and-a-flock-of-very-good-detectives/image-7.jpg)

A sheep whodunit. After Backrooms and Hokum I just needed something to pass the time, so I put it on without expecting much, and it was the best surprise of the week. Hugh Jackman plays a shepherd who reads murder mysteries to his flock every night, convinced they can't understand a word, right up until he turns up dead and the sheep decide they have to solve it. It's based on the novel Three Bags Full and directed by Kyle Balda in his first live action feature, and it's a brilliant little movie with a solid twist. **8.3/10.**

---

That's the full ride: a John Wick comfort watch, an Evil Dead franchise that couldn't decide if it wanted to scare me or lose me, two horror movies I actively regret, and a flock of sheep that ended up being the best thing I watched all week. Horror won't always deliver, but the movie you expect the least from sometimes will.
