The Furious is a Hong Kong action thriller directed by Kenji Tanigaki, a stunt coordinator turned director. It premiered at the Toronto International Film Festival's Midnight Madness section in September 2025 and opened wide worldwide on June 12, 2026.

It's led by Xie Miao as Wang Wei, a mute handyman whose quiet life gets torn apart when his teenage daughter Rainy is kidnapped by a child trafficking ring operating with police protection. Joe Taslim plays Navin, a journalist whose wife has gone missing while investigating the same network, and the two men team up out of shared desperation.

**A quick heads up: this review has spoilers**, including plot direction and a couple of specific moments from the third act. If you want to go in blind, bookmark this and come back after you've watched it.

---

## The kidnapping that kicks everything off

The setup moves fast, almost aggressively. Rainy gets lured away from a busy street in broad daylight, and the brazenness tells you upfront that whatever system is supposed to protect kids like her has already failed. Wang Wei and Navin's paths crossing feels less like a convenient plot device and more like two desperate men who've run out of other options. It's a strong hook.

## The action, and the moment my brain kicked back in

![The Furious First Fight Scene](/images/posts/i-think-the-furious-2026-is-overhyped/action-scene-1-.jpg)

This is where the movie lives up to its reputation, at least partially. The fight choreography is excellent. Every punch, throw, and improvised weapon strike looks like it hurts, and you can tell real stunt performers spent serious time rehearsing.

About twenty minutes in, I had to accept that these people are *functionally superhuman*, because no normal person survives this many hits and keeps fighting. Once I made peace with that, the question shifted from "is this realistic?" to "how crazy is this going to get?" and it became a lot more fun.

## Paklung deserved more screen time

![Joey Iwanaga as Paklung](/images/posts/i-think-the-furious-2026-is-overhyped/paklung.jpg)

Paklung is the film's villain, played by Joey Iwanaga, and he's my biggest issue with the movie, because I enjoyed him more than almost anyone else on screen. He doesn't get meaningful screen time until well over an hour in, with barely any setup before that, and then the third act arrives and it's "here's the big boss, look how unhinged he is."

Iwanaga is clearly having a blast playing against his usual, calmer roles. But a villain this interesting, someone hiding a fake identity behind a respectable businessman persona, deserved to be introduced and developed way earlier.

## The third act is where this movie either wins or loses you

![5 Way Fight](/images/posts/i-think-the-furious-2026-is-overhyped/5-way-de.jpg)

The final fight might be one of the best pure action sequences I've watched in a while. If you've watched Jujutsu Kaisen and remember the three-way domain expansion sequence, this is a five-way version of that everyone-fighting-everyone energy, with fists, blades, and improvised weapons instead of cursed techniques. There are arrows flying, knives changing hands mid-fight, and at one point two people fighting each other with bicycles. Ridiculous in the best way.

Not everything in the third act works, though. The villain's death scene felt underwhelming to me. I know some people will call it emotional, him reaching for a wife and child who are already gone, but it felt cheap.

---

## Is The Furious actually as good as the internet says?

If you've watched a reasonable amount of Asian action cinema, you know this movie is just okay by the genre's own standards, even with the high ratings. The fight choreography goes crazy, no argument, but fight scenes alone don't make a masterpiece. A lot of the online reaction seems to come from people seeing this level of stunt work for the first time, which is fair, but it's led to some seriously inflated claims about where this movie ranks.

---

## Final Verdict

**My Rating: 7.2**

The Furious is a fun, good time. It's not the genre-redefining masterpiece a chunk of the internet is treating it as, but it's not a movie you'll regret spending two hours on. Just go in knowing the story is thin, the villain needed more room to breathe, and you'll need to switch your brain off at some point.

*PS: this movie had me asking just one question during every single fight scene: **how are they still alive?***
