This list came together a little differently than my last one. A few of these I stumbled into through a random Twitter clip, one had so much hype behind it that I went in braced for disappointment and didn't get any, and a couple I only tried because I wanted to step outside my usual genre lane.

## 1. Interviews with Monster Girls (2017)

![Anime Recommendations: 5 Shows I Wasn't Expecting to Enjoy This Much](/images/posts/5-anime-i-wasnt-expecting-to-enjoy-this-much/interview-with-monster-girls.jpg)

**2017 · 1 Season · 12 Episodes (+ 1 OVA) · Completed**

Interviews with Monster Girls follows Tetsuo Takahashi, a biology teacher fascinated by "demis" – demi-humans like vampires, dullahans, and succubi living quietly among regular humans – as he gets the chance to study a handful of demi students at his school.

I saw a funny clip on Twitter and gave this one a shot, expecting a quick, forgettable comedy. I ended up loving it. What makes it work is the dynamic Takahashi has with his students. It explores what it's like to grow up different in a world that doesn't quite know how to accommodate you, and it's warm without ever tipping into anything uncomfortable.

**What could have been better:** I don't have any complaints. It's a short watch and it uses every one of its episodes well.

**Quick highlights:**

* **Takahashi and his students.** The heart of the show. Every interaction feels curious and kind, which a premise like this could have easily gotten wrong.
* **The demi cast.** Hikari the vampire, Kyouko the snow woman, Machi the dullahan, and Sakie-sensei the succubus each get specific insecurities tied to what they are, not generic teenage angst.
* **The tone.** Funny, gentle, and it never overstays its welcome.

## 2. Orb: On the Movement of the Earth (2024–2025)

![Anime Recommendations: 5 Shows I Wasn't Expecting to Enjoy This Much](/images/posts/5-anime-i-wasnt-expecting-to-enjoy-this-much/orb-on-the-movement-of-earth.jpg)

**2024–2025 · 1 Season · 25 Episodes · Completed**

The story follows a chain of people across generations in a fictional medieval Europe, all risking, and often losing, everything to prove heliocentrism, the idea that the earth moves around the sun, at a time when the church considers it heresy.

Where do I even start? This show got so much hype while it was airing, and if you ask me, it still wasn't enough. It was great from start to finish: the characters, the risks, everything they sacrificed for an idea most of them never lived to see vindicated. It made me appreciate the people who existed before us, named or unnamed, and how much invisible work went into getting humanity to where it is now. A must watch for everyone – anime fan or not.

**What could have been better:** My only real gripe is the last episode. The main story wraps up cleanly at episode 24, but episode 25 takes some getting used to, because it briefly threw off the closure episode 24 had already given me.

**Quick highlights:**

* **The generational structure.** No single protagonist carries the show. Watching the torch pass between people who never meet, all working toward the same forbidden idea, is devastating in the best way.
* **The sacrifice.** Almost nobody gets a happy ending, and the show never lets you forget what that cost.
* **The historical weight.** A real tribute to the unnamed people who pushed human knowledge forward long before anyone gave them credit.

## 3. My Happy Marriage (2023–2025)

![Anime Recommendations: 5 Shows I Wasn't Expecting to Enjoy This Much](/images/posts/5-anime-i-wasnt-expecting-to-enjoy-this-much/my-happy-marriage.jpg)

**2023–2025 (special coming Oct 2026) · 2 Seasons · 26 Episodes (+ 3-episode special) · Ongoing**

It's kind of a Cinderella story with a supernatural element, following Miyo Saimori, raised by an abusive stepmother, who gets married off to a cold, feared military officer named Kiyoka Kudo, only for the marriage to slowly turn into something she didn't see coming.

This one was a decent watch too. It's not my favorite on this list, since I'm not really a romance person, but I gave it a fair chance, and it's beautiful to watch once the story settles in. I'm sure romance fans will love it more than I did.

**What could have been better:** Season two doesn't quite hit the same emotional highs as season one. The stakes feel a bit more diluted once the central relationship stabilizes.

**Quick highlights:**

* **Miyo's arc.** Watching her go from someone who believes she deserves nothing to someone who slowly lets herself be loved is moving, even for a non-romance person like me.
* **Kiyoka.** The "cold military husband who's secretly gentle" archetype has been done before, but the show makes it work.
* **The supernatural backdrop.** It gives the romance just enough stakes and texture to stand out from a purely grounded period drama.

## 4. Kaguya-sama: Love Is War (2019–2022)

![Anime Recommendations: 5 Shows I Wasn't Expecting to Enjoy This Much](/images/posts/5-anime-i-wasnt-expecting-to-enjoy-this-much/kaguya-sama-love-is-war.jpg)

**2019–2022 · 3 Seasons · 37 Episodes (+ film) · On Hiatus, No Season 4 Announced**

Kaguya-sama follows two student council elites, Kaguya Shinomiya and Miyuki Shirogane, who are hopelessly in love with each other and far too proud to admit it first. So instead they wage an elaborate, ridiculous mental warfare campaign to force the other to confess.

I don't really watch romance, but I made an exception for this one because it's so well done. It's the comedy that gets me more than the romance. Watching them play mind games is endlessly entertaining, every scheme spirals more absurdly than the last, and somehow it never gets old. Ten out of ten, no notes.

**What could have been better:** Nothing about the show itself. My only complaint is that there hasn't been a new season announced in a while.

**Quick highlights:**

* **The mind games.** Every scheme between Kaguya and Miyuki is more elaborate and more delightfully petty than the last.
* **The comedy timing.** The visual gags, the internal monologues, and the over-the-top stakes given to the smallest social interactions all work consistently.
* **The slow-burn payoff.** Underneath the comedy, the actual feelings between the two leads are handled with real care whenever the show lets them show.

## 5. The Apothecary Diaries (2023–Present)

![Anime Recommendations: 5 Shows I Wasn't Expecting to Enjoy This Much](/images/posts/5-anime-i-wasnt-expecting-to-enjoy-this-much/the-apothecary-diaries.jpg)

**2023–Present (season 3 starting Oct 2026) · 2 Seasons · 48 Episodes · Ongoing**

It follows Maomao, a poison-obsessed apothecary's daughter sold into servitude at the imperial palace, who ends up solving medical mysteries among the palace's concubines and catches the eye of a mysterious, unusually beautiful official along the way.

I binged the entire first season in less than three days. The animation is gorgeous, easily one of the best-looking shows on this list, and the story underneath is even better. Maomao's sharp, dry outlook makes her one of the more fun protagonists I've followed in a while, and the medical mystery format gives each arc a hook of its own.

**What could have been better:** Very little. If anything, I wish I'd gone into it sooner instead of assuming it was going to be another forgettable palace drama.

**Quick highlights:**

* **Maomao.** An unconventional protagonist, driven by curiosity and a slightly unhealthy obsession with poisons rather than romance or ambition.
* **The mystery format.** Each arc gives her a real medical puzzle to untangle, keeping the show fresh episode to episode.
* **The animation.** Gorgeous, and easily one of the best-looking shows on the list.

---

Each of these brought something different, whether it was a dynamic I didn't expect to love, a romance I wasn't sure I'd care about, or a historical epic that left me thinking about the people history forgot. If you haven't seen any of them yet, give them a shot when you get the chance.
