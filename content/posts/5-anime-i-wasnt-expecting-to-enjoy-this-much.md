This list came together a little differently than my last one. A few of these I stumbled into through a random Twitter clip, one had so much hype behind it that I went in braced for disappointment and didn't get any, and a couple I only gave a chance because I was in the mood to step outside my usual genre lane. Give any of these a go in your spare time – you won't regret it.

- - -

### 1. Interviews with Monster Girls

![Interview-With-Monster-Girls](/images/posts/interview-with-monster-girls.jpg)

**2017 · 1 Season · 12 Episodes (+ 1 OVA) · Completed**

I saw a funny clip on Twitter and decided to give this one a shot, fully expecting nothing more than a quick, forgettable comedy. I ended up loving it. I really do. Interviews with Monster Girls follows Tetsuo Takahashi, a biology teacher fascinated by "demis" – demi-humans like vampires, dullahans, and succubi living quietly among regular humans – as he gets the chance to actually study a handful of demi students at his school.

What makes this show work is the dynamic Takahashi has with his students. It's beautiful to watch  and explores what it's like to grow up different in a world that doesn't quite know how to accommodate you. And it's warm without ever tipping into anything uncomfortable. It's a short, twelve-episode slice-of-life with a supernatural coat of paint, and it earns every bit of its charm.

**What could have been better:** Honestly, I don't have any complaints. It's a short watch and it uses every one of its episodes well.

**Quick highlights:**

* **Takahashi and his students.** The heart of the entire show. Every interaction feels curious and kind, which a premise like this could have easily gotten wrong.
* **The demi cast.** Hikari the vampire, Kyouko the snow woman, Machi the dullahan, and Sakie-sensei the succubus each get real, specific insecurities tied to what they are, not just generic teenage angst.
* **The tone.** It's funny, it's gentle, and it never overstays its welcome across its short run.

### 2. Orb: On the Movement of the Earth

![Orb-On-the-Movement-of-the-Earth](/images/posts/orb-on-the-movement-of-earth.jpg)

**2024–2025 · 1 Season · 25 Episodes · Completed**

Where do I even start? This show got so much hype online when it was airing and if you ask me, it still wasn't enough. The story follows a chain of people across generations in a fictional medieval Europe, all of them risking, and often losing, everything in pursuit of proving heliocentrism, the idea that the earth moves around the sun, at a time when the church considers that idea heresy.

It was great from start to finish. The characters, the risks they took, everything they sacrificed for an idea most of them never lived to see vindicated. It made me really appreciate the people who existed before us, named or unnamed, and just how much invisible work went into getting humanity to where it is now. It's a must watch for everyone – anime fan or not.

**What could have been better:** My only real gripe is the last episode. The main story wraps up cleanly at episode 24, but episode 25 takes some getting used to because it briefly threw off the sense of closure the 24th episode had already given me.

**Quick highlights:**

* **The generational structure.** No single protagonist carries the whole show. Watching the torch pass between people who never meet each other – all working toward the same forbidden idea – is devastating in the best way.
* **The sacrifice.** Almost nobody in this story gets a happy ending, and the show never lets you forget what that cost actually meant.
* **The historical weight.** It's a genuine tribute to the unnamed, unremembered people who quietly pushed human knowledge forward long before anyone gave them credit for it.

### 3. My Happy Marriage

![My-Happy-Marriage](/images/posts/my-happy-marriage.jpg)

**2023–2025 (special coming Oct 2026) · 2 Seasons · 26 Episodes (+ 3-episode special) · Ongoing**

This one was a decent watch too. Not my favorite on this list, since I'm not really a romance person, but I still gave it a fair chance. It's kind of a Cinderella story with a supernatural element woven in, following Miyo Saimori, raised by an abusive stepmother, who gets married off to a cold, feared military officer named Kiyoka Kudo, only for the marriage to slowly turn into something she didn't see coming.

It's actually beautiful to watch once the story settles in, and I'm sure romance fans will love it more than I did.

**What could have been better:** Season two doesn't quite hit the same emotional highs as season one. The stakes feel a bit more diluted once the central relationship stabilizes.

**Quick highlights:**

* **Miyo's arc.** Watching her go from someone who believes she deserves nothing to someone who slowly lets herself be loved is moving, even for a non-romance person like me.
* **Kiyoka.** The "cold military husband who's secretly gentle" archetype has been done before, but the show makes it work.
* **The supernatural backdrop.** It gives the romance just enough stakes and texture to stand out from a purely grounded period drama.

### 4. Kaguya-sama: Love Is War

![Kaguya-sama-Love-Is-War](/images/posts/kaguya-sama-love-is-war.jpg)

**2019–2022 · 3 Seasons · 37 Episodes (+ film) · On Hiatus, No Season 4 Announced**

I already mentioned I don't really watch romance but, I made an exception for this one because it's so well done. I think it's actually the comedy that gets me more than the romance itself. Kaguya-sama follows two student council elites, Kaguya Shinomiya and Miyuki Shirogane, who are both hopelessly in love with each other and both far too proud to admit it first, so instead they wage an elaborate, ridiculous mental warfare campaign to try and force the other to confess.

Watching them play mind games with each other is endlessly entertaining. Every scheme spirals more absurdly than the last, and somehow it never gets old. Ten out of ten, no notes.

My only complaint is that there hasn't been a new season announced in a while.

**What could have been better:** Absolutely nothing to point to here.

**Quick highlights:**

* **The mind games.** Every single scheme between Kaguya and Miyuki is more elaborate and more delightfully petty than the last. It never runs out of new ways to be funny.
* **The comedy timing.** The visual gags, the internal monologues, the over-the-top stakes given to the smallest social interactions, all of it lands consistently.
* **The slow-burn payoff.** Underneath all the comedy, the actual feelings between the two leads are handled with real care whenever the show decides to let them show.

### 5. The Apothecary Diaries

![The-Apothecary-Diaries](/images/posts/the-apothecary-diaries.jpg)

**2023–Present (season 3 starting Oct 2026) · 2 Seasons · 48 Episodes · Ongoing**

I binged the entire first season in less than three days. This is another fresh show in terms of premise, following Maomao, a poison-obsessed apothecary's daughter sold into servitude at the imperial palace, who ends up solving medical mysteries among the palace's concubines and catches the eye of a mysterious, unusually beautiful official along the way.

The animation is gorgeous, easily one of the best-looking shows on this list, and the story underneath it is even better. Maomao's sharp, dry outlook makes her one of the more fun protagonists I've followed in a while, and the medical mystery format gives each arc a real hook of its own.

**What could have been better:** Very little, honestly. If anything, I wish I'd gone into it sooner instead of assuming it was going to be another forgettable palace drama.

**Quick highlights:**

* **Maomao.** A unconventional protagonist, driven by curiosity and a slightly unhealthy obsession with poisons rather than romance or ambition.
* **The mystery format.** Each arc gives Maomao a real medical puzzle to untangle, keeping the show feeling fresh episode to episode.

- - -

Each of these shows brought something different to the table for me, whether it was a dynamic I didn't expect to love, a romance I wasn't sure I'd care about, or a historical epic that left me thinking about the people history forgot. If you haven't seen any of these yet, give them a shot when you get the chance.
