Genndy Tartakovsky's Primal debuted on Adult Swim in October 2019, spanning ten 22-minute episodes across its first season. Driven almost entirely by visual storytelling rather than spoken dialogue, the series centers on a caveman and a Tyrannosaurus rex fighting to survive in a harsh prehistoric landscape.

**A quick heads up: this review has minor spoilers**.

## What Worked

### The Friendship

Spear and Fang, a Tyrannosaurus, end up together because neither of them has anyone left. It is not an easy friendship. 
Fang steals his kills, eats his fruit even though she is a carnivore, and keeps him up at night with her breathing. It's funny, but also real: two creatures from completely different worlds trying to figure out how to exist side by side, and constantly failing at it.

### How The Story Is Told

There is no dialogue to hide behind and no exposition to lean on. It's just visual storytelling that hits you right in the chest every episode. Everything else comes through faces, body language, and sound, and I understood all of it. I felt every single thing these characters felt.

### The Tone

The show knows when to pull back, too. For a show this violent, the mammoth episode is one of the gentlest, saddest scenes of the season: a herd takes back a tusk and says a quiet goodbye to its dead. The blood moon episode goes the other way. It is chaos from start to finish, and it does not pretend to be anything else. Even the side story of a witch named Lula mourning her daughter gives the show a strange, sad humanity in the middle of all the monster stuff.

## What Didn't

I have no complaints. The show is exactly what it wants to be, and it does it well.

---

I randomly saw the trailer for the third season and decided to give the show a shot. Ended up binging the first season in one day. This is pure, intense and unhinged drama, stripped down to its rawest form.
