*Elite* is a Spanish teen drama from Netflix, created by Carlos Montero. It aired from October 5, 2018 to July 26, 2024, spanning 64 episodes in total. Set at Las Encinas, an exclusive private school, the show runs on class warfare, dead bodies, and a tangled web of secrets.

I binged all eight seasons after the show had already ended and jotted down my thoughts after every single one. Honestly, I wish I had been watching it live while it aired.

The short version: I loved the first three seasons, got worn down by the middle, enjoyed a solid rebound in Season 7, and left completely exhausted.

**A quick heads-up: this review contains spoilers** for all eight seasons. It also discusses abuse, sexual assault, and adults pursuing teenagers, since the show leans heavily on all three.

---

## The Good

### Seasons 1 to 3: Messy, addictive, and worth it

![Elite seasons 1 to 3](/images/posts/elite-full-retrospective/image-1.jpg)

**Season 1** was a great time. The pacing and mystery locked me in from start to finish. The class divide is the whole engine: rich kids get away with everything because the system is built to believe them, while working-class kids become easy scapegoats. Omar and Ander were easily my favorite part. While everyone else was scheming or spiraling, you'd cut to these two stealing a few quiet minutes together, and I was giggling and kicking my feet. Samuel didn't quite do it for me as the lead. He's easy to root for, just a little boring to watch.

The one thing I couldn't get past was Nano and Marina. Nano is a grown man and Marina is sixteen, yet the writers framed it as a forbidden romance with zero pushback from anyone and no acknowledgment that something was deeply wrong. I get that the plot needed Nano in Marina's orbit for the blackmail storyline, but there were other ways to get there. It soured a real chunk of the season for me, and it's the one creative choice I can't give the show a pass on.

**Season 2** was faster and messier, and it worked way better than it had any right to. Samuel going after Carla was the best thing the show had going. Then Polo confessed to Ander, which felt like relief for about five seconds before you realized what a disaster he had just unleashed. Watching that guilt hollow Ander out was painful, and Omar paid the price for it. On the downside, the writers had half-siblings Lu and Valerio kiss multiple times and treated it like edgy chemistry, which felt completely unnecessary.

**Season 3** felt like a natural series finale, and the writers nailed two big things. First was Polo. He was the saddest the show has ever made a character look, which is remarkable considering he's a murderer. Second was the earned friendships between Samuel and Guzman, and Nadia and Lu.

Everything else was a bit of a mess. Newcomers Malick and Yeray were basically plot devices with faces, and the writers stripped Carla of everything that made her dangerous. Meanwhile, Omar and Ander got dragged through a pointless cheating storyline that felt like a betrayal of everything the first two seasons built.


## The Bad

### Seasons 4 to 6: Where it lost me

![Elite seasons 4 to 6](/images/posts/elite-full-retrospective/image-2.jpg)

In **Season 4**, **the writers destroyed the best friendship on the show for a girl.** Samuel and Guzman had survived a murder cover-up together, yet the second Ari showed up, they were trading death stares in the hallway like strangers. It felt like the writers started fresh without reading their own past scripts. Guzman grew across three full seasons, and all that development was undone in two episodes for a love triangle that didn't even pay off. Ari simply wasn't a character worth burning a brotherhood over.

It went downhill from there. The new Blanco family felt predictable and cliché, because the writers seemed to think designer clothes and dark secrets counted as actual personalities. Omar and Ander, the couple that felt most real from Season 1, got blown up for cheap shock value. Armando was framed as a predator one minute and shot with moody, romantic lighting the next. Even the nudity stopped telling us anything about the characters and just became background noise. Mencía and Rebeka were the only dynamic that really worked. Worse, the finale was a lot of setup for a very soft landing, with all the tension gone the moment the premiere confirmed Ari survived.

**Season 5** was forgettable and genuinely tiring. Isadora spent her first few episodes defending Phillipe against assault allegations, only for the writers to subject her to an assault of her own. Then there's Cruz, a grown man pursuing a teenager all season, whose arc wrapped up with him "stepping aside" like it was some gracious, mature sacrifice. That's just a man stopping something he never should have started, and the show treated it like *growth*. It pissed me off.

Rebeka and Mencía were pulled apart and pushed back together so many times that I was numb by the finale, while Iván and Ari flip-flopped for eight straight episodes with no clear direction. The only moment that genuinely shocked me was Samuel turning up as the body in the pool at the end of episode four.

**Season 6** was soapy enough to stay watchable, with a few real highs. Cruz's storyline after coming out publicly showed the real cost of being a gay professional athlete in a homophobic environment, and Sara and Mencía's arc was a realistic look at a toxic, controlling relationship that unfortunately got buried under everything else.

The biggest issue was adding five new characters at once. Five! None of them got the screen time they deserved. The Ari, Patrick, and Iván triangle was a disaster, and the aftermath of Isadora's assault felt rushed.

Even the finale twist works in the moment and falls apart the second you think about it. Isadora House has a staffed parking lot and visible security, yet not a single camera catches who got into which car. Raúl supposedly bleaches the car interior, and nobody notices the fumes, not even Mencía when she wakes up inside it. The club was trivially easy to drug people in all season, and that convenient blind spot reappeared right when the writers needed it.

## How It Ended

### Seasons 7 and 8: A good stretch, then a messy goodbye

![Elite seasons 7 and 8](/images/posts/elite-full-retrospective/image-3.jpg)

**Season 7** was the best the show had been since Season 3, which I didn't think I'd be saying after that middle slump. Omar randomly comes back, Eric's mental health arc gets room to breathe because the writing actually takes it seriously, Iván shows real maturity, and Dalmar is a quiet highlight in a cast full of people barely holding it together.

It felt like a genuine step back toward what made the early seasons work. The dialogue was still cheesy, and the show completely dropped Omar's job running the student app after using it to kick off Eric's storyline, but for once the drama felt grounded in who these characters actually were.

Then came **Season 8**, a messy goodbye. Joel was treated like the season's true lead, pushing Sara, Nico, and Eric into the background during a final season that should have given everyone a proper sendoff. The first half juggled too many threads with no clear destination. The Alumni prostitution ring was introduced for maximum shock value and dropped just as fast, like the writers forgot it needed to go anywhere. And the incest angle between Héctor and Emilia by the pool didn't need to exist at all.

Once again, a grown adult chased a teenager: Detective Luis pursuing Isadora was deeply uncomfortable, and leaning on that trope for the umpteenth time didn't make it work. Making Luis Joel's killer didn't land for me either. The entire season built up Héctor's jealousy and Emilia's cold streak so one of them could be the culprit. Emilia had the motive, the ruthlessness, and the opportunity. She would have been a much stronger choice.

On top of that, Isadora fatally injecting Luis in plain view of arresting officers made zero sense, and Sara hitting Iván with her car back in Season 6 was never addressed again, not even in passing.

---

Looking back, it is obvious Elite overstayed its welcome, yet I couldn't bring myself to walk away. You don't sit through 64 episodes of chaos unless you genuinely love the show, and every new season came with that stubborn hope that maybe this would be the one where they got it right again. They fumbled the goodbye, and I am still mad about it, but I am glad I saw it through.

*PS: Nico was completely wasted in Season 8, and I still have no idea what Bilal's role was supposed to be in Season 6.*
