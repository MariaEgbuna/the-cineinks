Elite is a Spanish teen drama from Netflix, created by Carlos Montero. It premiered on October 5, 2018, and its eighth and final season arrived on July 26, 2024, for 64 episodes in total. It's set at Las Encinas, an exclusive private school, where class warfare, a dead body, and a web of secrets keep the story moving. I came to it after it had already ended, and I wrote down my thoughts after every season. Eight seasons later, I'm tired.

**A quick heads up: this review has spoilers** for all eight seasons, including who the Season 8 killer is, and it assumes you know the cast. It also talks about abuse, assault, and adults pursuing teenagers, since the show leans on all three.

## Seasons 1 to 3: Messy, addictive, and worth it

![REVIEW: Elite (2018-2024)](/images/posts/elite-full-retrospective/image-1.jpg)

**Season 1** was a great time. The pacing worked, the mystery worked, and I was locked in from start to finish. The class divide is the whole engine: rich people get away with things because the system is built to believe them, and poor kids become the easy scapegoat. Omar and Ander were easily my favorite part. Everyone else is scheming or spiraling, and then you cut to these two stealing a few quiet minutes together, and I was giggling and kicking my feet. Carla was terrifying in how calm and calculated she stayed while everyone around her fell apart. Samuel didn't do it for me as the lead. A few episodes in, I could tell you exactly what he'd do in any scene: the noble thing, the one that costs him. He's easy to root for, but boring to watch.

The one thing I couldn't get past was Nano and Marina. Nano is a grown man, Marina is sixteen, and the writers framed it as a forbidden romance, with no pushback from any other character and no acknowledgment that something is deeply wrong. I get that the plot needed Nano in Marina's orbit for the blackmail storyline, but there were other ways to get there. It soured a real chunk of the season for me, and it's the one choice I can't give the show a pass on.

**Season 2** was faster and messier, and it works more often than it has any right to. Samuel going after Carla was the best thing the show had going. Then Polo confessed to Ander, and it felt like relief for about five seconds before you realized what a mess he'd just made. Watching that guilt hollow Ander out was painful, and Omar paid for it. The writers also had Valerio and Lu, who are half siblings, kiss more than once and treated it like messy chemistry, which was unnecessary.

**Season 3** felt like a natural conclusion, and the writers got two things right. Samuel and Guzman went from enemies to each other's most reliable allies, a friendship built on grief and mutual respect, and Nadia and Lu finally chose each other too. Then came the finale, where the whole group stood together in a collective cover-up instead of throwing each other under the bus. After three seasons of betrayal, it was the only time the bond between these characters felt completely real. Polo at Las Encinas was the saddest the show has ever made a character look, which is remarkable given that he's a murderer.

Everything else was a mess. Malick and Yeray were plot devices with a face, and the writers stripped Carla of everything that made her dangerous. She spent two seasons as the most calculating person in any room, and Season 3 handed her a controlling father and a transaction dressed up as a boyfriend. Omar and Ander got dragged through a cheating storyline with no real point, and that felt like a betrayal of everything the first two seasons built.

## Seasons 4 to 6: Where it lost me

![REVIEW: Elite (2018-2024)](/images/posts/elite-full-retrospective/image-2.jpg)

**The writers destroyed the best friendship on the show for a girl.** Samuel and Guzman had survived a murder cover-up together, and then Ari showed up and the two of them were trading death stares in the hallway like strangers. It felt like the writers started fresh without reading their own previous scripts. Guzman grew across three full seasons, and all that work was undone in about two episodes for a love triangle that doesn't even pay off. Ari isn't a character you burn a friendship over.

It got worse from there. The new family were predictable and cliché, because the writers seem to think expensive clothes and family secrets count as personality. Omar and Ander, the couple that felt real from Season 1, got blown up for shock value. Armando was framed as a predator one minute and with charged music and a dark romantic glow the next. The nudity stopped telling us anything and became background noise. Mencia and Rebeka was the only thing that really worked. The ending was a lot of setup for a very soft bang, with the urgency gone the moment the show confirmed Ari was alive in episode one. The central mystery wrapped up with all the energy of someone quietly closing a browser tab.

Did I quit watching? No.

**Season 5** was forgettable, and it made me tired. Isadora defended Phillipe against assault allegations within her first few episodes, and then the writers put her through an assault of her own, which felt less like irony and more like nobody thinking it through. Then there's Cruz, a grown man pursuing a teenager all season, and the writers wrapped it up by having him "step aside" like it was a gracious, mature decision. That's a man stopping something he never should have started, and the show treated it as *growth*. It pissed me off. Rebe and Mencía were pulled apart and pushed back together so many times that I was exhausted by the finale, and Ivan and Ari flip-flopped for eight straight episodes with no throughline. The one moment that shocked me was Samuel turning up as the body in the pool at the end of episode four.

**Season 6** was soapy enough to stay watchable, with a few real highs. Cruz's storyline after coming out publicly showed what it costs to be a gay professional athlete in a homophobic environment, and Sara and Mencia was a realistic portrayal of a toxic, controlling relationship that got buried under everything else.

One major issue with this season was that the writers added five new characters at once. Five! None of them got the attention they deserved, and Nico's story about his surgery and identity, which had real potential, was squeezed into the final stretch. The Ari, Patrick, and Ivan triangle was a disaster, and the aftermath of Isadora's assault felt rushed. The finale twist works in the moment and falls apart when you think about it. Isadora House has a staffed car park and visible security, and not one camera catches who got into which car. Raul supposedly bleaches a car interior, and nobody notices the smell, including Mencia who woke up inside it. The club has been trivially easy to drug people in all season, and that same blind spot reappears exactly when the writers need it.

## Seasons 7 and 8: A good stretch, then a messy goodbye

![REVIEW: Elite (2018-2024)](/images/posts/elite-full-retrospective/image-3.jpg)

**Season 7** was the best the show had been since Season 3, and I didn't think I'd be saying that after the last few seasons. Eric's mental health arc got room to breathe because the writing treated it seriously. Ivan showed real maturity, and Dalmar was a quiet highlight in a season full of people barely holding it together. Carmen pushing Raul off that roof was a woman finally acting on years of watching men like him get away with it. It was a real step back toward what made the early seasons work. The dialogue was still cheesy, and the show dropped Omar's role running the student app after using it to build Eric's storyline, but for once the drama felt grounded in who these people are.

Then came **Season 8**, a messy goodbye. Joel was treated like the season's real main character, and Sara, Nico, and Eric got pushed to the background in a season that should've given everyone a proper sendoff. The first half juggled too many threads with no sense of where it was headed. The Alumni prostitution ring was introduced with maximum shock value and dropped just as fast, like the writers forgot it needed to go somewhere. The incest angle with Hector and Emilia at the pool didn't need to exist.

And Luis as Joel's killer just didn't work. The whole season builds Hector's jealousy and Emilia's cold streak so one of them can be the culprit. Emilia had the motive, the ruthlessness, and the opportunity. She would've been the stronger choice by a mile. Sara hitting Ivan with her car back in Season 6 never gets addressed, not even in passing. Isa killing Luis in full view of arresting officers makes zero sense when you think about it. And once again, a grown adult is chasing a teenager: Luis pursuing Isa is deeply uncomfortable, and it's not the first time the writers have leaned on that dynamic. It didn't work then and it still doesn't.

---

Elite definitely overstayed its welcome. I actually wished I was watching it while it aired. But here I am, still annoyed enough to write all of this, still holding onto what it was at its best.

*PS: Nico was completely wasted in Season 8, and I still have no idea what Bilal's role was supposed to be in Season 6.*
