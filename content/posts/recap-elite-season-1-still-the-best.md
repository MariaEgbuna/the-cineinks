Elite is what happens when you throw three broke kids into a school built entirely on money and secrets. It's messy, it's soapy, and I could not stop watching.

*This post contains spoilers for Season 1 of Netflix's Elite.*

*Content note: this recap mentions an adult sleeping with a 16-year-old, and a character's HIV status being outed in class.*

---

## How it kicks off

![RECAP: Elite Season 1](/images/posts/recap-elite-season-1-still-the-best/trio.jpg)

Samuel, Nadia, and Christian walk into Las Encinas as scholarship kids after their old school's roof caves in. The construction company behind the shoddy job, run by Teo and Ventura, both of whom have kids at Las Encinas, hands out the scholarships as a PR move.

Nadia hits the culture clash immediately: the school markets itself as multicultural, but she's told to take off her hijab on campus. Christian just wants in with the popular crowd. That's Marina and her brother Guzman, who draws the hardest line against the scholarship students; Lu, sharp and territorial, with something going on with Guzman; and Carla, who catches Christian's eye, though Ander, the principal's son, tells him she's been with Polo since they were twelve. Ander isn't rich and he's quietly closeted. He and Christian bond over weed that leads them to a dealer named Omar, Samuel's childhood friend, and when Ander finally meets someone off Grindr, it turns out to be Omar too.

At home, Samuel's brother Nano has just gotten out of prison. The two tag a house one night out of boredom, and it belongs to Ventura, the man funding the scholarships. It comes back to bite Samuel at Marina's sixteenth birthday party, held at that same house. That night, Christian's hookup with Carla turns out to be staged for Polo to watch, Guzman finds out about the tagging and threatens Samuel until Nadia shuts it down, and Marina tells her family flat out that she's not ashamed of anything, including being HIV positive. Then the show cuts to Marina in a body bag, and everything after is the road to how she ends up there.

## The blackmail plan

Nano owes money to men from prison. They hand him a gun and a name: shoot a man named Fer for missing a payment. Nano tracks Fer down, finds out he has a family, and can't do it. The thugs kill Fer themselves, stab Nano as a warning, and raise his debt from forty thousand euros to sixty. Marina is the one who patches him up while Samuel is in the other room, unaware.

Meanwhile, Marina forgets the camera is rolling during a school project with Samuel and admits on tape that her father is sitting on documents proving the construction company cut corners on the school that collapsed. Nano finds the footage on Samuel's laptop and sees a chance to blackmail. Samuel is horrified, but with his brother stabbed and drowning in debt, he agrees. Marina ends up making the theft possible. She lifts a set of house keys and the alarm code from Carla's place so Nano can clear out her father's watch collection, and he walks out with all of it, including a red-banded watch that holds the evidence.

Nano and Marina sleep together, and Marina realizes she's actually in love with him. She starts avoiding Samuel without explanation. Then a teacher confiscating a phone reads her HIV status out loud in front of the whole class. That's when we learn how she got it: at fourteen, from a boy named Pablo who didn't know he had it. Her parents didn't press charges. They paid Pablo to disappear and quietly let slide that Guzman, Polo, and Ander had beaten him nearly to death, which is why the police later look at Guzman as a suspect.

Marina collapses, ends up in the hospital, and her pregnancy comes out. Samuel assumes it isn't his and becomes obsessed with finding the father, not realizing it's Nano. Marina considers ending the pregnancy, but Nano talks her out of it, promising the watch money will be enough to start over somewhere else. Samuel's hunt goes wrong when he wrongly accuses a teacher and attacks him in front of the principal. Nadia clears it up and exposes a blackmail arrangement between that teacher and Lu, which gets the teacher fired and Lu suspended.

## The love stories underneath

Nadia and Guzman get paired for a school project, exactly as Lu wanted. It comes out that the two of them made a bet: Guzman could get Nadia into bed. Nadia finds out, humiliated. Guzman keeps showing up anyway, gives her a custom hijab as an apology, and fumbles an attempt to grovel to her parents that accidentally exposes the fact that the school banned her hijab. He also finds his own father doing lines of cocaine at a family event and decides to protect him instead of exposing him. Nadia decides he's grown into someone worth forgiving.

Polo reveals he's known about Christian and Carla's staged hookup all along, then pulls Christian into a threesome with Carla. He lands Christian a modeling opportunity, then quietly sabotages it out of jealousy once things look real between Christian and Carla. Carla is furious, and after Christian finally asks to be her actual boyfriend, she breaks things off with Polo for good.

Ander and Omar get forced into the open the hard way. Guzman, angry after a fight with Nadia, outs Omar to her as both gay and a dealer. Nadia struggles with it given her family's faith, which stings Omar enough that he cuts Ander off. It doesn't last. Even after Ander's mother catches them kissing and Omar's father, once he learns the truth, decides to force Omar into an arranged marriage, the two keep finding their way back to each other.

## How it all wraps up

![RECAP: Elite Season 1](/images/posts/recap-elite-season-1-still-the-best/s1-finale.jpg)

It all blows up by the end of episode seven. Carla can't prove Marina and Nano are behind the blackmail, but she tells her father, who tells Ventura, and Marina has to admit she's pregnant with Nano's child. Guzman, blindsided, pulls in Ander and Polo to go after Nano, but it's Samuel who throws the first punch, and it turns into a full brawl. Guzman hides at Nadia's family shop, Polo slips away, and Ander is beaten badly enough that Omar finds him bleeding in an alley.

By the finale, Marina's father plans to force an abortion the next morning, so she and Nano decide to skip town for Asilah in Morocco and raise the baby there. That night is graduation, and Marina wins the school's top scholarship trophy by default, since Nadia has been pulled out of school by her father and Lu is suspended, leaving neither able to sit their exams. She slips away to wait for Nano by the pool. Samuel finds her crying and tries one last time to talk her out of leaving, and she says no. He begs Guzman to talk his sister down, but Guzman brushes him off.

Alone at the pool, Marina gets a visit from Polo, who's spiraling and convinced that getting the watch back is his only shot at winning Carla back. She won't hand it over, and whatever she says cuts deep enough that he hits her with the trophy she just won. He runs to the locker room and walks in on Christian and Carla mid hookup. Carla gets the whole story out of him and goes straight into damage control: she forces Christian to help build an alibi, finds Polo a clean shirt, and the three of them walk back out to the party like nothing happened.

Nano reaches the pool and finds Marina dying. She falls into his arms and doesn't make it. He calls for help that never comes, panics, and runs, which is exactly what Samuel sees. Samuel's account makes Nano the prime suspect, Polo sticks to Carla's story, and no one looks twice at him. Guzman, wrecked with grief, blames Samuel for ever introducing Marina to Nano, and Samuel reminds him he begged for help hours earlier. The next day, Lu finds Guzman on the edge of a bridge and talks him down. Nano is arrested for murder, Polo walks free, and the trophy that killed Marina sinks to the bottom of a reservoir, buried along with the truth.

## Where we left everyone

* **Polo:** Free. Not a scratch on him.
* **Carla:** Untouchable. She ran an entire murder cover-up in real time and her world is exactly how she designed it.
* **Christian:** Trapped. He's an accessory now, and Carla holds that over him.
* **Nano:** In prison for a murder he didn't commit.
* **Samuel:** His crush is dead, his brother is behind bars, and he's the reason the police looked at Nano.
* **Guzman:** Wrecked with guilt and grief. Lu talked him down from a bridge.
* **Nadia:** Pulled out of school by her father, missing her own exams.
* **Ander:** Badly beaten in the brawl and found bleeding in an alley by Omar.
* **Omar:** Still with Ander, but facing a forced marriage his father is arranging to bury the shame of him being outed.

## What worked and what didn't

Omar and Ander were easily my favorite part of the season. Their chemistry felt so real, and their scenes were the only moments I got to giggle and kick my feet. Everyone else is scheming or spiraling, and then you cut to these two stealing a few quiet minutes together. The Carla, Polo, and Christian storyline was a highlight too. Watching Carla operate was fascinating. She's terrifying in how calm and calculated she stays while everyone around her falls apart.

Samuel didn't do it for me as the lead. A few episodes in, I could tell you exactly what he'd do in any scene: the honest thing, the noble thing, the thing that costs him. There's no contradiction in him, which is why he attacks an innocent teacher over a hunch. That makes him easy to root for and uninteresting to watch. For a show built on a dead body and a mystery, your lead should be pulling you forward, not sitting still while everyone else does the heavy lifting.

I went into the Lu and Guzman bet rolling my eyes. A bet to get someone into bed is a tired, gross setup, and normally I'd check out. But credit where it's due, the show kinda pulled it off. It turned something lazy and mean-spirited into an actual redemption arc, so I'll let this one slide.

The thing I couldn't get past was Nano and Marina. Nano is a grown man in his twenties, Marina is sixteen, and the show frames it as a forbidden romance. The writing glosses over it, with no pushback from any other character and no acknowledgment that something is deeply wrong. I get that the plot needs Nano in Marina's orbit for the blackmail storyline, but there were other ways to get there that didn't romanticize this. It soured a real chunk of the season for me, and it's the one choice I can't give the show a pass on.

## Overall thoughts

As a whole, Season 1 held up well. The pacing and the mystery structure worked, and I was locked in from start to finish. The class divide is the whole engine: rich people get away with things because the system is built to believe them, and poor kids become the easy scapegoat.

**My Rating: 8.3**
