Hope is completely insane, and I didn't mind one bit.

It's the latest film from Na Hong-jin, the director of The Wailing, and it competed for the Palme d'Or at Cannes in May. It runs around 160 minutes and stars Hwang Jung-min, Zo In-sung, and Hoyeon, with Michael Fassbender and Alicia Vikander in the cast too. I liked it enough to watch it twice. I rewatched it with my brother, and we still had a blast.

The movie is set in Hope Harbor, a remote village near the heavily fortified DMZ. Police chief Bum-seok and officer Sung-ae get called out when a mysterious creature wrecks the village, and a group of hunters, including Sung-ki, goes into the forest to track it and ends up being hunted instead.

The first hour plays like a grounded horror mystery. A creature is tearing through a small village, the hunters head into the forest, and for a while it feels like a very tense creature movie. Then, after the first hour and half, it goes full chaotic sci-fi, and it keeps escalating until the end.

It's a lot. I know it divided people at Cannes, but I never minded. The switch is so big that I just went along for the ride, and it's why the rewatch with my brother was as fun as the first time.

I did leave with questions, though. The townspeople have guns, and I couldn't work out how. I went online and found out it's because Hope Harbor sits right next to the DMZ. Most of what I understand about this movie, I picked up online.

That's partly because the movie doesn't explain much. Na has said the original script was too long and too dense for a single movie, so he cut it down. I figured that meant no sequel, which is why he ended up explaining so much himself in interviews. He's actually said a sequel script is already written, but nothing's confirmed, so we'll see.

## Final Verdict

**My Rating: 7.3**

If you like your movies big and a little unhinged, watch this one. Bring someone to react with, because you'll want to talk about it right after.
