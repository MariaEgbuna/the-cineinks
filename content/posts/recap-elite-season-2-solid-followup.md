Season 2 picks up directly from season 1 and doesn't let you forget for one second how completely rigged the whole thing was.

Season 1 ended with the truth sitting right there in plain sight, and the wrong person still went to prison for it.

Nano's behind bars for a murder Polo committed, Carla's holding the cover-up together, and Samuel's grieving, furious, and running out of legitimate options. And into all of that chaos, Las Encinas welcomes three new students who each bring their own special brand of disaster.

As usual, flash-forwards drop you back into police interrogations while the season slowly walks you toward another body.

_This post contains spoilers for Season 2 of Netflix's Elite._

---

### How it kicks off

Guzman's family loses their house before the opening credits even finish rolling. Rebeka's family, poor turned suddenly rich, moves into it, and Rebeka enrolls at Las Encinas the same day Guzman has to walk past his own old front door and pretend it doesn't hurt. He's not handling any of it well. Still raw over Marina, he attacks Samuel on his first day back and the two of them go straight to blows.

Valerio shows up not long after, Lu's half-brother, the kind of person she can't leave alone in a room without something catching fire. He announces himself by inviting the entire school out to his VIP table at a club that night, and pretty much everyone takes him up on it.

Christian's barely holding together under the weight of what he knows. He's the only one aware that Nano's in prison for a murder Polo actually committed, and Carla's spent the summer trying to keep him distracted enough not to act on it. It's not working. She can feel him slipping toward the truth, and so can we.

At the club, things split in two directions. Nadia, Samuel, and Rebeka actually get a night off, and Nadia tries alcohol for the first time. Meanwhile Christian reaches his breaking point and tells Carla that he's going to the police. She'd already warned her father this might happen, and he'd told her not to worry about it. Furious and drunk, Christian storms off on his motorcycle and gets hit by a car that doesn't stop. He survives, but barely, and Carla's father shows up at the hospital offering to fly him to Switzerland for treatment, a gesture that's really just a payoff dressed up as generosity.

While all that's unfolding, Omar and Nadia's father has a stroke back at the family store. Nadia's phone is off the whole time she's out celebrating, so she has no idea until Ander rushes her to the hospital. Ander, trying to explain who he's and why he's the one bringing her, completely fumbles the introduction to her mother and ends up leaving mortified.

Back at Valerio's, he and Lu end up drunk and alone together, and it tips into an actual kiss before Lu catches herself and pulls away, reminding him they swore this would never happen again. He's her brother. The look on his face says he's not over it.

Then the episode cuts to something that changes everything you just watched: a police search moving through a forest, calling out a name, looking for a Las Encinas student who never made it home in his uniform. It's Samuel.

---

### The twists and the escalation

Samuel decides early that Carla's the way in, and he starts working her deliberately. It doesn't take long before he gets somewhere he didn't expect: at a club, he asks her if she had something to do with Marina's death, and she says yes. The confession somehow tips straight into them sleeping together, which becomes the pattern for the next few episodes. Every time Samuel corners her for real answers, it ends the same way, and every time he wakes up with less information than he wanted.

Around them, everyone else is falling apart in their own way. Guzman can't process his sister's death and starts drinking and using with Valerio, who's more than happy to enable him. One drunken night at a party, Guzman blurts out to the group that Marina was murdered, planting a seed that starts eating at everyone. Polo, meanwhile, is slowly falling apart under the weight of what he actually did. He starts hallucinating from guilt, develops a pill addiction, and eventually breaks and tells Ander the truth. Ander doesn't take it well, and now he's carrying a secret heavy enough to wreck him and his friendship with Guzman too. It gets so bad that Polo tries to drown himself in the school pool, and it's Cayetana, of all people, who pulls him out and covers for him without having any idea what he's actually guilty of.

Ander's relationship with Omar buckles under all of it. Omar wants to be public about them, but his father's disapproval and then Polo's secret pull Ander in different directions until he's too depressed to function. Things get worse at home too: Omar finally snaps at his father and gets thrown out, ending up living with Ander indefinitely. It's a rare bright spot buried in a rough stretch for both of them, and it doesn't stay simple for long either, since Ander can't tell Omar what's actually wrong with him.

Nadia and Guzman drift toward each other almost by accident, born out of Guzman needing someone steady and Nadia being unable to stay away. They finally sleep together, and Guzman uses it as part of his reason for finally ending things with Lu for good. Lu doesn't take the breakup well.

Cayetana's double life finally cracks open too. She's been performing wealthy heiress all season while her actual mother cleans for a living, including Rebeka's own mansion and the school. Rebeka figures it out and confronts her directly. Cayetana's answer says everything about her: nobody was ever going to hand her the life she wanted, so she built the illusion herself. Polo ends up being the one person she lets see the truth, and something starts forming between them, even as her mother loses her job over Cayetana's stealing and the family's finances collapse further.

The Halloween party's where things start to boil over. Samuel and Rebeka have been quietly building a case, piecing together old photos that put Polo in Christian's shirt the night Marina died, inching closer to the truth without fully seeing it yet. Nano, who was let out due to lack of evidence, shows up uninvited wearing a mask, and corners Carla, convinced she's manipulating his brother. Samuel finds them and snaps, beating Nano badly enough that it becomes one of the last things anyone can pin down before he goes missing.

By episode six, Samuel's confident enough in what he knows to confront Guzman directly and tell him Polo's behind Marina's murder, with Christian somehow involved too. Guzman nearly starts a fight over it. Not long after, Nano goes to see Marina's mother to insist on his innocence, and when Guzman finds out, he goes looking for blood. He catches up to Samuel in the street and starts beating him down, and it's only when Samuel plays back a recorded confession from Carla that Guzman actually stops.

Everything goes down for real at Cayetana's charity event in episode seven. What was supposed to be a small fundraiser to quietly bail out her family spirals into a massive gala, and Lu, humiliated after discovering she'd been played, uses her speech in front of every wealthy parent in town to blow up everyone's secrets at once: Cayetana's lie, Guzman and Nadia's affair, and Carla sleeping with Samuel, all in one breath. The party implodes. Cayetana runs off devastated, Lu escalates further and later posts a video of Nadia and Guzman having sex online out of pure spite, and Guzman corners Ander to ask, point blank, if Polo killed his sister. Ander lies straight to his face. The episode ends with Samuel riding home alone through the woods at night, a car closing in behind him in the dark.

---

### How it all wraps up

Nadia and Guzman spend the fallout from Lu's speech as the school's favorite gossip, humiliated by a sex tape. Valerio admits he's the one who recorded it, which doesn't make things easier for Nadia, who ends up telling her own family there's a video out there that could shame them. They tell her to forget it and never bring it up again.

The search for Samuel keeps tearing the group apart. His bike and blood-soaked jacket turn up in the woods, and everyone starts turning on each other, throwing out theories and suspicion in every direction. Carla goes as far as asking her own father outright if he had something to do with it. He denies it, unconvincingly. Rebeka, meanwhile, blames her mother, certain that dragging Samuel into her illegal side business is what got him killed.

Christmas dinner's where Lu's chickens come home to roost. She can't help bragging about everything she exposed at the charity gala, and Valerio, disgusted, retaliates by outing their own sexual relationship to their father. The fallout's immediate and ugly, and Valerio tells Lu that as far as he's concerned, she's nothing to him now except a _sister_.

Polo, in the middle of all this, shows up at Cayetana's with a grand gesture: he's gotten her back into school, gotten her mother's job back, and hands over enough money to cover their immediate expenses, all under the idea that everyone deserves a second chance.

Then the show finally pulls back the curtain on where Samuel's been. In flashback, Guzman reaches out and tells Samuel he actually believes his story, and Samuel asks to meet somewhere quiet. On his way there, he's chased down a deserted road by men in a car, barely making it to Guzman, who hides him until the danger passes. That close call becomes the seed of a plan: Samuel disappears into Guzman's grandparents' country house, and Guzman plants the bloody jacket to make it look like something worse happened. The whole point is to pressure Carla into cracking under the guilt of thinking she got him killed.

It works almost too well. The Inspector eventually gets a warrant for the property, and while searching it, finds Samuel very much alive in the boiler room. Instead of blowing the whole thing open, she walks out and claims she saw nothing, which turns out to be because she's in on it too, using the fake disappearance to manipulate Carla into confessing. And Carla does. She goes to the police and tells them everything. Samuel comes out of hiding with a flimsy story about a rave, and the truth about the staged disappearance stays buried, at least for now.

Right before the police can move on Polo, he finally admits the murder to Cayetana but she doesn't flinch. She repeats his line about second chances back to him, and it's clear from how she says it that she's already thinking about how to make the evidence disappear. Polo gets arrested at school not long after, and the fallout splits the group even further: Guzman finds out Ander knew the truth the entire time and cuts him off completely. Cayetana, true to her word, is shown hiding the murder weapon in her closet. In custody, Polo denies everything.

Omar's packing to leave Ander for good, done with months of watching him shut down and go apathetic with no explanation, when he finally learns the real reason: Ander's been carrying Polo's secret this whole time. It reframes everything Omar's been angry about, and instead of walking away, he forgives him.

By the time school resumes after New Year's, Guzman and Samuel seem to have made peace, at least on the surface. Then Polo walks back into school like nothing happened, released from custody with no explanation given. Only Cayetana looks happy to see him. Behind him, a new student is already talking to the principal, and the season ends on that note, one door quietly closing while another one opens.

---

### Where we left everyone:

*   **Polo:** Free again. Cayetana hid the evidence that would've kept him locked up.
*   **Carla:** She confessed to the police and it meant absolutely nothing. That has to be its own kind of devastating.
*   **Nano:** Cleared, but a wrongful arrest doesn't just evaporate. He packs his bags and disappears in the rain, gone before the season even ends.   
*   **Samuel:** Got his brother out, but the method cost him more than he expected, including whatever he and Carla actually became to each other.    
*   **Ander:** Spent the whole season hollowed out by Polo's secret, but Omar finally learns the truth and forgives him. They end the season back together.   
*   **Cayetana:** Burned her integrity to belong somewhere.   
*   **Valerio:** Still here. Still underdressed for an academic institution. Still somehow everyone's problem.
    
---

### What worked and what didn't

The real backbone of Season 2 is Samuel going after Carla, and it was the best thing the show had going. It just works.

Christian just vanishing from the show partway through bugged me while I was watching. He gets shipped off to Switzerland after one episode and the show barely mentions him again, which felt like a character just getting dropped. Turns out that's real world scheduling catching up with the story: Miguel Herrán, who plays Christian, was juggling this with Money Heist at the same time, and the show clearly wrote him out to make room for it. Doesn't fully excuse how abrupt it feels on screen, but it explains it.

The Valerio and Lu thing was rough to watch. They're half siblings, they kiss more than once, and the show treats it like messy sibling chemistry. There's no real reckoning with how wrong it is, just a "we promised we wouldn't do this again" line and then more scenes of them being pulled toward each other anyway. I get that the show loves pushing boundaries, but this one was unnecessary.

The show does a solid job of showing how guilt with nowhere to go just starts rotting everything around the person carrying it. When Polo finally breaks and confesses to Ander, it feels like relief for about five seconds before you realize what a massive disaster he just created. Because now Ander knows. And that secret's too heavy to carry and too dangerous to pass on, especially to Omar. Watching it hollow Ander out across the season was just painful. The warmth just drains out of him completely. And the biggest casualty of all of it is his relationship with Omar, taken apart piece by piece by something Omar isn't even allowed to know about.

Cayetana, the girl who spent the entire season desperately performing like she belonged at Las Encinas, finally crossed the line into actually becoming one of them: through covering up a murder. That's either brilliant writing or a coincidence that happened to work out perfectly.

---

### Overall thoughts

Season 2 was actually decent. It's faster and a little messier than Season 1, but it works more often than it has any right to. You just have to accept upfront that none of these people behave like actual teenagers. Make your peace with that and it's a gripping watch.

The real question heading into Season 3 is whether the show can hold this kind of tension now that Polo's the known quantity rather than the hidden one. **A secret's only dangerous while it's still a secret.** Now everyone knows. What does the show do with that?

My Rating: **8.5**
