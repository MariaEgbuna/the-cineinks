Dorohedoro Season 2 took six years to arrive, and every second of the wait was worth it. Drop everything and go watch it.

It picks up exactly where the first season left off, with no hand-holding and no boring recap episode. MAPPA and director Yuichiro Hayashi stepped right back into this unhinged world like no time had passed. The season ran 11 episodes from April 1 to May 27, 2026, and I waited for the whole thing to drop before hitting play. It was the best call I could have made. Watching this week to week would have broken me.

New here? Start with [my Season 1 quick take](/posts/review-dorohedoro-season-1-underrated).

## More of absolutely everything

Everything that made me fall in love with the first season got amplified here. The action is nastier, the character drama has real emotional punch, and the twists hit you from angles you will never see coming. And the gore? If you thought Season 1 was intense, this season says doesn't hold back.

The narrative leans heavily into the **Cross-Eyes faction**, that chaotic gang we saw circling Caiman and Nikaido's story in the first finale. Diving into their internal mess, their desperate obsession with their missing boss, and how they collide with En's sorcerer family gives this season a massive sense of scope that Season 1 just didn't have the real estate for. There are *a lot* of moving pieces this time around:
*   **Shin and Noi** get their own fantastic character arc.
*   **Risu** is out here bloodily chasing his own answers.
*   **Caiman and Nikaido** are right in the thick of it.

Amazingly, none of it feels overwhelming. The show trusts you to keep up, and the payoff is massive.

## What happened to the CGI growing pains?

Remember how I said the 3D animation took a minute to get used to in Season 1? Yeah, forget about that. **There is zero adjustment period this season.** Whatever rendering tweaks MAPPA made behind the scenes over the last six years, it completely paid off. The character movement is significantly smoother, the chaotic action sequences land with actual physical weight, and the visual style never once pulled me out of the zone. It owns its unique look now.

## Does the plot have any complaints?

I have zero issues with the storytelling. Every single thread this season pulls on connects right back to the bigger picture of Caiman's dark past and why he ended up with a lizard head in the first place. The flashbacks hit way harder because we're deeply invested in these characters by now. It is incredibly tight writing from the first episode to the last frame.

## Should you binge it?

I am so incredibly glad I binged all 11 episodes. The pacing is phenomenal, with no mid-season dips and no stalling episodes where you start checking your phone. An anime that can maintain this kind of breakneck momentum for a full season is incredibly rare, but *Dorohedoro* pulled it off flawlessly.

It beautifully balances pitch-black humor with genuine psychological dread. The extreme violence never feels gratuitous, and every single character beat pays off something planted years ago.

And here is the absolute best news: **we are not waiting another six years for the next ride.** Season 3 was officially announced right after the finale dropped, and word is it's already deep in production. No more agonizing multi-year gaps!

---

This season is exactly what happens when a creative team knows what a show wants to be and executes it without a single second of hesitation. If Season 1 was the brilliant setup, Season 2 is the show fully realized.

*PS: This season will absolutely have you deep-diving into internet theory threads to catch the things you missed on your first pass. Don't be surprised if you end up rewatching a few episodes before you can finally move on.*
