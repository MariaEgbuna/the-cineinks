Season one was about two grieving creatures finding each other in the wreckage of everything they lost. Season two takes that and throws it across oceans and centuries.

This was a wild ride from start to finish, full of surprises I did not see coming.

_This post contains spoilers for Primal Season 2._

* * *

### Lost at sea, and then lost from each other

![RECAP: Primal Season 2](/images/posts/recap-primal-season-2/image-2.jpg)

Season two picks up right where season one left off. Mira has just been taken away on a ship, and Spear is not about to let that be the end of it. He tries to swim after the boat and fails, washing right back up on the same beach he started from. So he does the only other thing he can: he builds a raft. Fang is not thrilled about getting on it, and only does once Spear bribes her with bugs.

The two of them ride the open ocean together, fishing for food, watching shooting stars and a lunar eclipse pass overhead, and surviving entirely on each other. A massive shark destroys their raft in a storm, Spear ends up inside its mouth before stabbing his way out, and by the time the seas calm down, Fang has washed up alone on an unfamiliar beach with no idea where Spear is.

The split sets up the next two episodes. Fang, stranded and searching, runs into a red Tyrannosaurus who keeps his distance at first before the two start hunting and bonding together. Spear, meanwhile, gets dragged inland by a group of strangers and wakes up with a broken arm in the middle of what is basically a Celtic village. He fights his way out before their leader steps in and proves they mean him no harm.

The collision between these two storylines is brutal. Red and Fang, now bonded, end up raiding the same village that just took Spear in. Spear sees Fang with a body in her mouth and has no idea what to think. A three way standoff turns into a full battle between Spear, Red, and the entire village, and it ends with Fang killing Red to protect Spear, something she clearly does not want to do. She comforts Red as he dies, and that single moment says more about who Fang has become than anything else in the two episode arc. Spear watches all of it happen, wounded and shaken, and the two of them walk back into the forest together, both carrying something heavier than before.

### New faces, old ghosts, and a war with the Vikings

![RECAP: Primal Season 2](/images/posts/recap-primal-season-2/image-3.jpg)

Still shaken by what happened with Red, Spear and Fang take shelter in a cave during a storm, where Spear finds a wall of old paintings showing humans, dinosaurs, and creatures from long before either of them. It is a quiet stretch. Spear even copies Mira's nightly moon ritual at one point, without really understanding why he is doing it.

A bear carrying a Viking warrior crashes into their cave, and Spear spots the scorpion symbol on the warrior's shield, the same one branded on Mira. From there it is a straight line of violence: Spear and Fang track the Viking back to his village, free Mira and a group of other captives, and immediately find themselves at war with the whole settlement.

The fight that follows is one of the most savage in the whole series. Fang kills a Viking woman named Rikka by slamming her into the ground, and in the chaos, Spear accidentally kills her child after the boy latches onto his back. He throws the kid off without realizing what he has done until it is already too late. Spear, Fang, and Mira escape on a stolen boat while the village burns behind them, and the surviving Viking Chieftain and his remaining son set out for revenge with nothing left to lose.

A side episode steps away from the main story to follow a group of Victorian era scientists in 1890s England, one of whom ends up living out Spear's entire journey almost beat for beat after being attacked by an escaped, feral inmate. It almost feels like a what if: what would happen if a regular man got pushed back to the same primal instinct Spear has been running on this whole time.

Back with the main cast, the Viking Chieftain and his son catch up to Spear, Mira, and Fang at sea, and the fight ends in tragedy on both sides. Fang throws Eldar overboard and Spear knocks his father off a flying bird mount mid-battle, but Spear ends up accidentally killing Eldar in the fall while trying to save his own father. The Chieftain, having lost everything now, simply gives up and lets the river carry him away.

What he finds downstream is worse than death. Dragged into an actual underworld of fire and lava, he is offered a deal by a massive, horned demon: power and revenge, in exchange for whatever is left of his soul. He takes it without blinking, and the season's real villain is born.

Back with Spear, Fang, and Mira, life gets gentler for a moment. Fang lays a clutch of eggs, and Spear is clearly thrilled about it, fishing nonstop to keep her fed and giddily helping build her nest. The Chieftain, now a fire demon, attacks, and though Spear and Mira manage to fend him off, the bigger threat turns out to be the next group that finds them: a massive Egyptian war ship that takes the whole group prisoner, smashing one of Fang's unborn eggs in the process.

### Slavery, empires, and a giant named Kamau

![RECAP: Primal Season 2](/images/posts/recap-primal-season-2/image-4.jpg)

The middle stretch of the season is where Primal goes the biggest it has ever gone. Spear, Mira, and Fang are dragged aboard the Colossaeus, a literal floating city run by a ruthless Egyptian Queen who forces captives into endless war campaigns across the ancient world. They are not alone. A massive, enslaved giant named Kamau, the one who accidentally smashed Fang's egg, ends up locked up right alongside them, and despite the obvious tension, the four of them are forced into an uneasy team just to survive.

What follows is basically a tour through history's worst battles, with Spear, Fang, and Kamau forced to fight in invasion after invasion: a Babylonian kingdom, a Qing Dynasty city, an early Slavic settlement, a Greco-Roman city. Every win just means another battle waiting right behind it, over and over, with no end in sight for any of them. Mira, meanwhile, is forced to dance for the Queen's entertainment, which is honestly one of the more painful threads of the whole season to watch.

Kamau's backstory finally gets told. He was a peaceful farmer before the Queen's army ambushed his village, killed his people, and forced him into servitude using his daughter as leverage. It changes how you see everything he has done up to that point, including the egg he destroyed by accident, and it sets up the rebellion that closes out this stretch of the season.

That rebellion is huge. Spear, Mira, Fang, and Kamau finally turn on the Queen and her entire fleet, freeing the enslaved rowers below deck along the way. Kamau gets the most satisfying moment of payback in the whole season, throwing the Queen overboard to her death after she spends three episodes holding his daughter hostage. The group escapes with Fang's two surviving eggs intact, but the celebration does not stretch on for long. In the distance, the Viking Chieftain, now a literal demon of fire, has finally caught up to them.

### Spear's ending

![RECAP: Primal Season 2](/images/posts/recap-primal-season-2/image-5.jpg)

The final episode pulls all the way back to Spear's childhood for the first time in the whole series, showing his father leading their tribe before dying defending it from a pack of smilodons, with young Spear forced to finish the fight and take his father's place as leader. Looking back at it now, you can see exactly why Spear became who he is: a man who has been losing family and stepping into the role of protector since before the show even started.

From there, the group makes landfall, and Mira finally reunites with a friend from her old life, Amara, leading Spear, Fang, and her two surviving offspring into Mira's actual village. Spear struggles to find his place among other humans for the first time since losing his own family, eventually retreating into his new home to paint his entire life story on the walls using his own blood. Mira finds the mural the next morning and sees that Spear painted everyone else surrounded by family, while he drew himself standing alone off to the side.

The Viking Chieftain catches up to them one last time, now fully transformed by the fire demon's power. Spear and Fang fight him together, and Fang manages to put herself out after being set on fire, but Spear charges in anyway, tackling the Chieftain down a mountainside and taking fatal burns in the process. The Chieftain survives and gloats over his win before getting dragged back into the underworld by the same demon that gave him his power in the first place.

Spear is brought home unconscious, and the village shaman makes it clear there is nothing more to be done. In his final moments, Mira looks at his mural and finally understands what he wanted all along: a family, and a place to belong. She climbs onto him, and the two spend his last moments together before he dies.

The season closes years later, with Fang and her now grown offspring still living in Mira's village, and a teenage girl, Spear and Mira's daughter, riding one of Fang's children and letting out a roar of her own as the screen cuts to black.

* * *

### Where we left everyone

*   **Spear:** Died defending Mira's village from the Viking Chieftain, after finally finding the sense of family and belonging he had been chasing since losing his first family in season one.
*   **Fang:** Survived the Chieftain's attack, raised her surviving offspring to adulthood, and remains in Mira's village years after Spear's death.
*   **Mira:** Lost Spear but carried on his legacy, raising their daughter within her own community alongside Fang and her offspring.
*   **The Viking Chieftain:** Survived his final fight with Spear, dragged back into the fiery underworld by the demon that gave him his powers in the first place.
*   **Kamau:** Freed himself and his daughter from the Egyptian Queen's enslavement, last seen sailing toward an unknown future after years of servitude.
    
* * *

### Final verdict and overall thoughts

I went into this season already knowing Mira was Spear's love interest. That was never in question for me. What I did not expect was how big the show would go to get there: new tribes, new civilizations, an entire ancient world I had no idea was coming. That is the real strength of this season, it kept widening the map without ever losing the two characters at the center of it.

Where it lost me a little was the ending. It felt rushed, and from what I have heard, that comes down to the show not knowing if it would even get a third season while this one was being made. You can feel that uncertainty in how fast everything wraps up. Still, even with that stumble, the story this season told was a great one, and it lands as bittersweet in the best and worst ways.

**My Rating: 8.7**
