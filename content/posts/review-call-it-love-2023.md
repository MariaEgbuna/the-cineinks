*Call It Love* (also released as *Say It's Love* in some regions) is a 2023 South Korean romantic melodrama that premiered on Disney+ on February 22 and wrapped up on April 12 that year. Lee Sung-kyung stars as Shim Woo-joo and Kim Young-kwang as Han Dong-jin, with Sung Joon as Yoon Joon and Kim Ye-won as Woo-joo's older sister. It was directed by Lee Kwang-young, written by Kim Ga-eun, and produced by Studio Dragon and Drama House.

A quick heads up before I go further: **this review has minor spoilers.** Nothing that ruins the ending, but I do talk about plot direction and pacing in enough detail that you might want to bookmark this and come back after you've started the show.

---

## The Premise

Woo-joo's life falls apart when her father dies. He had a mistress on the side, one who then has the audacity to kick Woo-joo and her family out of their own home. Understandably furious, Woo-joo decides she wants revenge, and she zeroes in on Han Dong-jin, the mistress's son, as her target. The problem is that Dong-jin turns out to be nothing like the smug, spoiled rich kid she expected. He's gentle, lonely, and dealing with his own baggage, and the longer Woo-joo spends around him plotting her payback, the more she starts falling for him instead.

It's a familiar setup if you've watched any amount of Korean melodrama. Revenge plot collides with a slow-burn romance, misunderstandings pile up, everyone's too polite to just say what they're feeling until episode eleven. Call It Love leans hard into the "gentle soul healing a wounded soul" dynamic rather than going for spectacle, which is exactly where my feelings about this show start getting complicated.

## What Worked

### Kim Young-kwang

![Kim Young-kwang as Han Dong-jin in Call It Love](/images/posts/review-call-it-love-2023/kyk-1-.jpg)

I've seen Kim Young-kwang in a handful of things before this, and every single time, he's been cast as some flavor of villain. He played a terrifying psychopathic killer in **Somebody** back in 2022, and more recently he showed up as a menacing crime figure in **Trigger**. Even outside the shows I've personally watched, his reputation online leans heavily toward "the guy who plays unsettling bad guys really, really well". So watching him play Dong-jin, a soft-spoken, emotionally guarded but fundamentally kind man, was a lovely surprise. (If you want to see the villain side, he's also in my [K-drama recommendations](/posts/kdrama-recommendations-may-27-2026) for *Evilive*.)

Honestly, he's the entire reason I decided to watch this show in the first place, and even with all my complaints about the rest of it, I don't regret that decision. His performance alone justified the time.

### The Supporting Cast

![The cast of Call It Love](/images/posts/review-call-it-love-2023/cil-cast.jpg)

It's not just Kim Young-kwang and Lee Sung-kyung doing the heavy lifting here. Sung Joon plays Yoon Joon, a friend caught in his own tangle of unspoken feelings, and while his arc mostly exists to add a layer of will-they-won't-they tension on the sidelines, he brings a warmth to the role that keeps those scenes from feeling like pure filler. Kim Ye-won, playing Woo-joo's older sister, gets pulled into a parallel storyline involving the decades-old rivalry between their mother and the woman who broke up their parents' marriage in the first place. 

That older generation's drama runs quietly underneath the main romance the whole time, and there were stretches where I found myself more curious about how that older feud would resolve than I was about the central couple, especially once the main romance started running out of new ground to cover.

## What didn't work

### Sixteen episodes is too many for this story

![A scene from Call It Love](/images/posts/review-call-it-love-2023/cil.jpg)

The story feels stretched well past the point where it had anything left to say. Maybe that's on me for signing up for a melodrama and being surprised it moved slowly, since that's the genre's whole personality. But even allowing for that, sixteen episodes is a lot of runtime for a story this thin.

I waited until around episode twelve for the romance to become properly official, and the second I got it, I started losing interest. It also became obvious the show was stalling, not building toward anything new. Twelve episodes, maybe thirteen, with the repetitive miscommunication scenes trimmed, would have hit so much harder. Instead, the final third circles ideas the show had already resolved.

### The revenge plot fizzles out

This is probably my biggest gripe, and the one that confused me most. The show opens with Woo-joo swearing revenge on the family that wronged hers, and I expected something with real teeth: telenovela-level scheming, sabotage, someone publicly humiliated at a company gala. What I got was Woo-joo taking a job at Dong-jin's company and doing nothing with it. The revenge angle is set up with real weight in the opening episodes, then quietly deflates as the show turns its attention to the romance.

I understand the point: empathy can dissolve the need for revenge once you get to know the person you wanted to hurt. That's a nice idea for a melodrama. But if you open your whole show on a revenge premise, you need to *actually do something with it* before swapping it for longing looks and unspoken feelings. As it stands, it feels like a hook to get you invested before the pivot to pure romance. I don't understand why so many people rate this drama so highly when one of its two core premises barely gets developed.

---

## Should You Watch It?

This show is almost entirely carried by the romance and the acting. If either of those had been weaker, this would have been a much rougher watch given how much patience the show demands. I'd still recommend to someone specifically chasing a character-driven melodrama, or anyone curious about Kim Young-kwang outside of his usual villain roles, but go in with a trimmed set of expectations for the revenge angle and a decent amount of patience banked for the final stretch.
