A true crime mystery with a great premise, a stacked cast, and somehow still no idea where it's going.

_**The Watcher**_ was created by Ryan Murphy and Ian Brennan for Netflix, and it stars Naomi Watts and Bobby Cannavale as a couple who buy their dream house and start getting threatening letters from someone who signs off as "The Watcher." It's a 2022 show billed as horror and mystery thriller, and it's loosely based on a real unsolved case out of New Jersey.

That true story angle is exactly what pulled me in. A family gets stalked by an anonymous letter writer who claims to have been watching the house for generations, and to this day nobody knows who sent those letters. It's a strong premise for a series. In practice, I finished all seven episodes disappointed.

### The Plot

The Brannocks move into their new house and the letters start almost immediately. From there the show throws suspect after suspect at you: the neighbors, a private investigator, a cult theory, a guy connected to a decades old murder case. Every episode adds another layer instead of building on the one before it.

By the middle point, the show stops feeling like it's building toward anything. New characters and new theories get introduced, then get dropped without much follow-up. It's less a mystery unraveling and more a mystery that keeps replacing itself.

The last two episodes try to pull everything together, and that's where it falls apart the most. Storylines that got a lot of screen time earlier barely get mentioned again. The ending doesn't resolve who The Watcher actually is, which I get, since the real case is unsolved too. But there's a difference between an intentional open ending and a show that just runs out of road.

### What worked:

The cast. Naomi Watts and Bobby Cannavale sell the paranoia well, and Jennifer Coolidge and Mia Farrow show up and immediately make every scene they're in more fun to watch. The performances are the one part of this show that never dips.

The premise itself still works too. A stalker who's been "watching" a house for generations and won't say why is creepy on its own, no embellishment needed. The first couple episodes lean into that and build real tension.

### What didn't:

The writing tries too hard to be clever. Every episode wants to be the one with the twist, and after a while the twists stop meaning anything because there's no line connecting them. It felt like the writers were chasing "shocking" instead of "coherent", and it just didn't work.

I almost dropped it after episode three but I pushed through since it was only seven episodes, but that shouldn't be the bar a show has to clear.

### Rating

**5.1** out of 10.

If I had to sum this show up in two words: **frustratingly stupid.**

I don't think this is a bad watch exactly. It's decent, the cast is doing real work, and the premise is solid. But for something riding on a genuinely unsettling true story, it ends up average at best, and average is a disappointing place to land after all the hype it got online. This is easily the weakest thing I've watched this month, because it wastes a good premise on a plot that never commits to a direction.

_PS: if you've seen this one, I'd love to know if you figured out or have an idea about who The Watcher was supposed to be before the finale, because I couldn't tell you._
