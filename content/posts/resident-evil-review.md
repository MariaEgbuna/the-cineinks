A rollercoaster of jump scares, unexpected comedy and human reactions. Resident Evil proves that sometimes an ordinary guy trying to get home makes for the best horror protagonist.

Directed by Zach Cregger and written alongside Shay Hatten, Resident Evil (2026) is a new entry in the long-running horror franchise. Starring Austin Abrams as Bryan, alongside Paul Walter Hauser, Kali Reis and Zach Cherry, the movie takes us back to the infamous Raccoon City.

The story follows Bryan, a medical courier trying to navigate a new junction in his personal life: whether or not he's ready to become a father. While he and his girlfriend struggle with that decision, an unusually lucrative delivery offer comes his way. All he has to do is deliver a package to Raccoon City. Sounds simple enough. But these things never are.

**A quick heads up: this review has minor spoilers**, including details about Bryan's infection and character development, but I'll avoid revealing the ending.

## Your everyday Joe in a zombie apocalypse

![REVIEW: Resident Evil (2026)](/images/posts/resident-evil-review/image-2.jpg)

One of my biggest takeaways from this movie is how refreshing it was to have an ordinary person as the protagonist.

We don't get an athletic Black Ops operative with raw edge, military training and an arsenal of weapons. We get Bryan, a regular guy who finds himself in a situation he never signed up for and is desperately trying to survive long enough to get back home.

His reactions are probably my favourite part of the experience. He's frightened, confused and occasionally makes questionable decisions. And why wouldn't he? Your everyday Joe isn't exactly a Midwest gunslinger. He's not suddenly an expert at handling weapons or fighting infected creatures. He's simply trying to stay alive.

The comedy also deserves its flowers. It comes from Bryan's reactions, the absurdity of the situations he finds himself in and some genuinely amusing dialogue. Even the most tense encounters occasionally become funny without making you forget that he's fighting for his life. The balance is surprisingly good.

## The infected keep things unpredictable

![REVIEW: Resident Evil (2026)](/images/posts/resident-evil-review/image-3.jpg)

Another highlight is the infected themselves. Their ability to merge with other infected and replace damaged body parts makes encounters considerably more unpredictable.

You're not simply dealing with ordinary zombies that can be taken down with a few well-placed shots. Their unusual abilities introduce another layer of danger, and Bryan's lack of experience makes these encounters even more entertaining to watch.

The journey towards Raccoon City is particularly effective. What starts as a simple delivery quickly turns into a fight for survival, with Bryan discovering that the surrounding areas are just as dangerous as the destination itself.

Once he finally reaches the city, the objective becomes relatively straightforward: get from point A to point B. Considering the outbreak has only been going on for a few hours, I thought the setting served its purpose without needing unnecessary detours.

## There's more to Bryan than survival

![REVIEW: Resident Evil (2026)](/images/posts/resident-evil-review/image-4.jpg)

Something I appreciated was how the movie handled Bryan's uncertainty about becoming a father.

At the beginning, he's clearly struggling with the decision. However, after becoming infected and gradually accepting that he might not survive, his perspective changes. It's only when he begins to lose the possibility of having a future that he realises he actually wants a family.

It's a simple but effective character development that gives his journey a little more emotional weight.

Umbrella Corporation also plays an important role in the story, particularly when Bryan discovers the significance of the cargo he's transporting. However, I couldn't help feeling that the corporation's motives deserved a little more exploration. There's an interesting story there, and I would have liked to see more of it.

## I wasn't ready for it to end

My biggest complaint about this movie is that it was simply too short!

I genuinely wouldn't have minded another 15–30 minutes. Not because I think a particular section needed fixing or that the story required unnecessary additions, but because I was enjoying the experience enough to want more of it.

The movie delivers a surprisingly entertaining combination of horror, comedy, suspense and emotional storytelling. More importantly, it manages to make its protagonist relatable without sacrificing the danger surrounding him.

I'm not particularly a Resident Evil fan, but I walked away wanting a sequel. That says quite a bit about how well the movie managed to pull me into its world.

---

## Final Verdict

**My Rating: 8.5**

Resident Evil is a thoroughly enjoyable horror experience that understands the value of an ordinary protagonist, well-timed humour and unpredictable threats. It might have ended sooner than I would have liked, but if this is the direction the franchise is taking, I'd happily take another trip to Raccoon City.
