Let me tell you about the Roundup movies, the most criminally slept-on action franchise on the planet right now.

The Roundup series is a South Korean action-crime juggernaut built around one man: Detective Ma Seok-do, played by Ma Dong-seok, also known as the Monster Cop, and the carnage that follows him everywhere. His team is perpetually one crisis away from a breakdown, the villains are menacing, and Ma walks through all of it like a force of nature, slapping, throwing, and demolishing anyone dumb enough to get in his way. Gambling rings, drug cartels, cross-border syndicates: they all underestimate him, and they all pay for it.

We're four films deep with a fifth already confirmed, and this franchise has no business being this consistently good. It's classic buddy-cop energy mixed with hard-hitting modern action choreography. The four films have drawn over 40 million moviegoers, making it the first Korean film franchise in history to reach that number.

*This post contains minor spoilers for the Roundup Korean movies.*

---

## The Plot (In a Nutshell)

Detective Ma Seok-do is a beast of a cop with zero patience for criminals and an uncanny ability to stumble into disasters above his pay grade. Each film drops him into a new international crisis and a new class of villain. The cases change. The carnage does not.

## The Roundup Movies in Order

![The Roundup Movies in Order: Korea's Most Slept-On Action Franchise](/images/posts/hall-of-fame-the-roundup-movies/image-2.jpg)

**THE OUTLAWS (2017):** This is where it all started, and it's so damn good it almost feels unfair. Directed by Kang Yoon-sung in his feature debut, it's based on a real 2004 gang conflict in Seoul's Garibong-dong neighbourhood, dubbed the Heuksapa Incident. Detective Ma is trying to keep a fragile peace between local gangs in Chinatown when a savage new crew from Yanbian, China blows it up with a bloody turf war. Yoon Kye-sang plays the villain, Jang Chen, and he's unhinged enough that you tense up every time he's on screen. Kang Yoon-sung won Best New Director at both the Korean Association of Film Critics Awards and the Baeksang Arts Awards, and both wins were deserved. It set a bar the rest of the franchise has been chasing ever since.

![The Roundup Movies in Order: Korea's Most Slept-On Action Franchise](/images/posts/hall-of-fame-the-roundup-movies/image-3.jpg)

**THE ROUNDUP (2022):** Ma heads to Vietnam to bring back a small-time suspect and walks straight into something far worse: a ruthless killer targeting South Korean tourists. This sequel matches the original almost beat for beat in quality and intensity. It became the top-grossing Korean film of 2022 with 12.69 million admissions, the first Korean film to pass 10 million viewers since before the pandemic. Choosing between this and The Outlaws for the top spot is one of the hardest calls you'll make as an action fan.

![The Roundup Movies in Order: Korea's Most Slept-On Action Franchise](/images/posts/hall-of-fame-the-roundup-movies/image-4.jpg)

**THE ROUNDUP: NO WAY OUT (2023):** The weakest entry, and it hurts to say that because the action still slaps. Ma joins a new metropolitan unit chasing a dangerous synthetic drug through the Seoul club scene, in a story based on a real 2017 to 2018 methamphetamine smuggling case involving South Korea, Japan, and Taiwan. The main villain never quite clicks and feels underbaked, and the secondary villains, a lethal Japanese Yakuza cleanup crew, are so much more menacing that every scene without them is a step down. It still pulled 10.68 million admissions in Korea. Worth watching, but clearly the odd one out.

![The Roundup Movies in Order: Korea's Most Slept-On Action Franchise](/images/posts/hall-of-fame-the-roundup-movies/image-5.jpg)

**THE ROUNDUP: PUNISHMENT (2024):** The bounce-back the franchise needed. It premiered at the 74th Berlin International Film Festival, and Ma goes after a massive illegal online gambling operation running out of the Philippines, with a cryptocurrency money laundering angle. It broke Korean film presale records, selling over 834,000 tickets in advance, and reached 10 million admissions faster than any previous entry. Kim Moo-yeol plays a ruthless ex-special forces killer who's menacing in the way the third film badly needed, and the action set pieces are some of the best in the franchise.

## The Action: Raw and Masterfully Shot

I can't move on without talking about how these fights are filmed, because they're operating on a different level from most Western action cinema right now.

No lazy shaky-cam. No rapid cuts every half-second to hide that nobody can really fight. The camera steps back and lets you see everything: every open-hand slap, every shoulder throw, every hit from Ma Dong-seok coming down with full bone-crunching weight. He's built for exactly this, and the choreography is designed around what his body actually does instead of cutting around what it can't. The action director on the first film, Heo Myeong-haeng, went on to direct the fourth, which tells you how much the choreography is the backbone of the whole operation.

## Why It Works

The Roundup series knows exactly what it is and never apologizes for it. These aren't films trying to be prestige cinema in disguise. They want you to feel the hits, root for the good guy, and walk out satisfied, and they're actually well-made instead of cynically assembled.

Ma Seok-do is also refreshing as a protagonist. He's not morally conflicted, he's not broken, and he's not hiding a secret that gets revealed in the third act. He's a cop who loves his job, is very good at it, and solves problems with his hands. There's something deeply satisfying about a character who sees a problem and just deals with it.

## Final Verdict

I'm completely locked into this franchise for life. Watch the films in order, because the first two are essential. Power through the third. Let the fourth remind you why you started. With a fifth already confirmed, there's no better time to get caught up.

Hall of Fame. Stop sleeping on it.
