Let me tell you about the Roundup movies, the most slept-on action franchise on the planet right now.

It's a South Korean action-crime series built around one man: Detective Ma Seok-do, played by Ma Dong-seok, the Monster Cop. Gambling rings, drug cartels, cross-border syndicates: they all underestimate him, and they all pay for it.

We're four films deep with a fifth already confirmed, and the four films have drawn over 40 million moviegoers, making it the first Korean film franchise to reach that number. It has no business being this consistently good.

*This post contains minor spoilers for the Roundup movies.*

## The Plot (In a Nutshell)

Ma Seok-do is a beast of a cop with zero patience for criminals and a talent for stumbling into disasters above his pay grade. Each film drops him into a new international crisis and a new kind of villain. The cases change. The carnage does not.

## The Franchise Breakdown

![The Roundup Movies in Order: Korea's Most Slept-On Action Franchise](/images/posts/hall-of-fame-the-roundup-movies/image-2.jpg)

**THE OUTLAWS (2017):** Where it all started, and it's so damn good it almost feels unfair. Kang Yoon-sung directed it in his feature debut, based on a real 2004 gang conflict in Seoul's Garibong-dong neighbourhood, called the Heuksapa Incident. Ma is keeping a fragile peace between Chinatown gangs when a savage new crew from Yanbian, China starts a bloody turf war. Yoon Kye-sang plays the villain, Jang Chen, and he's so unhinged you tense up every time he's on screen. Kang won Best New Director at both the Korean Association of Film Critics Awards and the Baeksang Arts Awards, and both were deserved. It set a bar the rest of the franchise has been chasing since.

![The Roundup Movies in Order: Korea's Most Slept-On Action Franchise](/images/posts/hall-of-fame-the-roundup-movies/image-3.jpg)

**THE ROUNDUP (2022):** Ma goes to Vietnam to bring back a small-time suspect and walks into something worse: a ruthless killer targeting South Korean tourists. It matches the original almost beat for beat in quality and intensity. It was the top-grossing Korean film of 2022 with 12.69 million admissions, the first Korean film to pass 10 million viewers since before the pandemic. Choosing between this and The Outlaws for the top spot is one of the hardest calls an action fan has to make.

![The Roundup Movies in Order: Korea's Most Slept-On Action Franchise](/images/posts/hall-of-fame-the-roundup-movies/image-4.jpg)

**THE ROUNDUP: NO WAY OUT (2023):** The weakest entry, and it hurts to say that because the action still slaps. Ma joins a new metropolitan unit chasing a dangerous synthetic drug through the Seoul club scene, in a story based on a real 2017 to 2018 methamphetamine smuggling case involving South Korea, Japan, and Taiwan. The main villain feels underbaked, and the secondary villains, a lethal Japanese Yakuza cleanup crew, are so much more menacing that every scene without them is a step down. It still pulled 10.68 million admissions. Worth watching, but clearly the odd one out.

![The Roundup Movies in Order: Korea's Most Slept-On Action Franchise](/images/posts/hall-of-fame-the-roundup-movies/image-5.jpg)

**THE ROUNDUP: PUNISHMENT (2024):** The bounce-back the franchise needed. It premiered at the 74th Berlin International Film Festival, with Ma going after a huge illegal online gambling operation in the Philippines, with a cryptocurrency money laundering angle. It broke Korean presale records with over 834,000 advance tickets and reached 10 million admissions faster than any previous entry. Kim Moo-yeol plays a ruthless ex-special forces killer who is menacing in the way the third film needed, and the action set pieces are some of the best in the franchise.

## The Action: Raw and Clearly Shot

These fights are filmed on a different level from most Western action cinema right now. No lazy shaky-cam. No cuts every half-second to hide that nobody can really fight. The camera steps back and lets you see everything: every open-hand slap, every shoulder throw, every hit from Ma Dong-seok coming down with full bone-crunching weight.

The choreography is designed around what his body actually does, not cut around what it can't. The action director on the first film, Heo Myeong-haeng, went on to direct the fourth, which shows how much the choreography is the backbone of the whole operation.

## Why It Works

The series knows exactly what it is and never apologizes for it. These aren't films trying to be prestige cinema in disguise. They want you to feel the hits, root for the good guy, and walk out satisfied, and they're well made instead of cynically assembled.

Ma Seok-do is also a refreshing protagonist. He isn't morally conflicted, he isn't broken, and he isn't hiding a secret that comes out in the third act. He's a cop who loves his job, is very good at it, and solves problems with his hands. There's something satisfying about a character who sees a problem and just deals with it.

## Final Verdict

I'm locked into this franchise for life. Watch the films in order, because the first two are essential. Power through the third. Let the fourth remind you why you started. With a fifth already confirmed, there's no better time to get caught up.

Hall of Fame. Stop sleeping on it.
