I have a few things sitting on my chest about the state of television and film right now. Instead of screaming into the void on social media, I figured I’d dump it all right here. 

To be clear: this isn’t a blind rage post. This is a carefully curated collection of industry trends that have been quietly eroding my sanity for the last few years. 

Let's discuss.

## The Death of Momentum: Why Does One Season Take Three Years?

*Severance* is the show that officially broke my spirit on this. Season 1 wrapped its masterpiece of a cliffhanger in April 2022. We didn't get Season 2 until January 2025. Nearly three entire years for ten more episodes of a mystery show that relies entirely on momentum. I know the 2023 Hollywood strikes scrambled production timelines everywhere, but this "three years between seasons" metric is becoming the baseline for television, and it's killing the medium.

Then there is the *Blue Eye Samurai* situation, which is arguably even worse. Season 1 dropped to massive acclaim in November 2023. As it stands right now, Season 2 isn't slated to hit Netflix until sometime in 2027. That is a brutal four-year gap. The creators have openly stated that the staggering level of animation takes an immense amount of time, and look — I respect the artistry. I want things to be good. But at a certain point, the wait actively works against the art. Hype evaporates, people move on, and by the time the new episodes finally load, you basically have to re-watch the original season just to remember who everyone is and why you cared in the first place.

## Atmospheric Murk: Where Did the Lighting Go (And Why Can’t I Hear Anyone)?

Can someone please explain to me when it became industry standard for entire scenes to be shot in pitch-black darkness? I understand the concept of mood lighting. I appreciate a shadow. But there is a massive difference between intentional cinematic chiaroscuro and simply failing to light a set properly. 

Too many modern shows lean on digital darkness as a lazy shortcut for "atmosphere," and it just results in a murky, compressed, unwatchable mess on consumer TVs. Classic Hollywood movies managed to build spine-chilling tension and moody noir vibes without forcing the audience to squint at a black screen trying to differentiate between two moving silhouettes. 

To make matters worse, the audio mix is suffering from the exact same identity crisis. Dialogue is constantly muffled, buried under a wall of bass, or completely drowned out by an aggressive musical score and explosive sound effects during action sequences. I shouldn't need a high-end soundbar and a degree in audio engineering just to decipher basic conversational dialogue in a multi-million dollar production. When you combine pitch-black visuals with incoherent audio, it stops looking like "prestige art" and just starts looking unpolished.

## Stop Subsidizing the Celebrity Voice-Acting Grift

Leave it to the actual, trained voice actors. I am saying this with my entire chest. Hollywood has developed an exhausting obsession with casting massive, A-list marquee names in animated projects solely for the marketing value of putting their name on the poster. The problem? Most of these actors don't actually voice *characters* — they just stand in a booth, read lines in their normal speaking voice, and collect a massive check. It completely shatters the illusion.

There are exceptions to the rule, of course. J.K. Simmons always gets a lifetime pass. Yes, he sounds exactly like J.K. Simmons when he’s voicing Omni-Man in *Invincible*, but his natural register carries the precise gravity and menace that the character physically demands. More importantly, he actually has vocal range. When you look at his work as Kai in *Kung Fu Panda 3*, Mayor Lionheart in *Zootopia*, or Klaus, none of them sound like the exact same guy. He bends his performance to suit the animation.

On the flip side, Awkwafina needs to be banned from the recording booth indefinitely. I am entirely spent on seeing her name attached to animated projects. She has voiced Sisu in *Raya and the Last Dragon*, Scuttle in *The Little Mermaid*, Chump in *Migration*, and Zhen in *Kung Fu Panda 4* — and every single performance is just her regular, unchanged speaking voice with zero attempt at vocal characterization. It is distracting. Give these roles back to the absolute geniuses who actually train for voice work, who know how to manipulate their pitch and pacing, and who actually disappear into a character instead of just showing up as themselves.

## A Public Accountability Check: Navigating the MCU Backlog

To close this out on a more positive note, I am putting this in writing so I am legally obligated to follow through: I am finally tackling the Marvel Cinematic Universe streaming backlog. 

I successfully avoided the vast majority of the Disney+ MCU television slate for years. The only exceptions I made were for *WandaVision*, *She-Hulk*, and the recent *Wonder Man* series. But the cultural tides have shifted a bit lately, and the craving for pure, comic-book superhero content has officially hit me again. I’m not going to fight the mood.

I don’t have a concrete strategy on whether I’m going chronologically or just picking whatever thumbnail looks coolest first, but consider this my public commitment. If I drop off the face of the earth, assume I am buried somewhere under Phase Four.
