I have a few things sitting on my chest about the state of entertainment right now, and I figured instead of complaining into the void, I'd just write it all down here.

None of this is a rage post. It's a collection of things that have been quietly annoying me for a while now.

---

## Why does one season take three years now?

**Severance** is the one that broke me on this. Season 1 ended in April 2022, and season 2 didn't premiere until January 2025. That's nearly three years for ten more episodes of a show that had already hooked everyone. Part of the gap came down to the 2023 writers' and actors' strikes, but even so, three years is a long time to hold an audience on a show built around mystery and momentum.

**Blue Eye Samurai** might be worse. Season 1 dropped in November 2023, and as of now, season 2 isn't expected until 2027. That's closer to four years, and the creators have said the animation alone takes an enormous amount of time. I get that quality takes time, but at some point the wait starts working against the show. People move on, hype fades, and when the new season finally shows up, you're starting from scratch trying to remember who everyone was and why you cared.

## Where did all the lighting go, and why can't I hear anyone?

Somebody needs to explain when it became normal for entire scenes to be so dark you can't tell what's happening. Mood lighting has its place, but there's a difference between intentional darkness and just not lighting a scene properly. Too many shows and movies lean on darkness as a substitute for atmosphere, and it ends up looking murky and cheap instead of moody. Older films managed tension without turning the whole frame into a black screen with faint outlines moving around in it.

The sound has the same problem. Dialogue comes out muffled, or the mix is off, especially in action scenes where the score and effects drown out the conversation. It shouldn't be a struggle to understand basic dialogue in a professionally produced show. Paired with the lighting, it makes some productions feel less polished than they actually are.

## Celebrities need to stop voicing animated characters

Leave it to the real voice actors. I say this with my whole chest. Too many big name celebrities get cast in animated projects for the marquee value of their name, and most of them don't shape their voice into the character. They just sound like themselves reading lines, and it takes you right out of it.

J.K. Simmons gets a pass though. He voices Omni-Man in **Invincible**, and yes, he sounds like himself, but his voice carries the weight the character needs. Some actors sound like themselves because their natural register fits the role perfectly. Others sound like themselves because they didn't put in any effort. Simmons has also voiced Kai in **Kung Fu Panda 3**, Mayor Lionheart in **Zootopia**, and the lead in **Klaus**, and none of them sound like the last.

Awkwafina should never voice anything ever again, and I don't want to see her in general right now. She's voiced Sisu in **Raya and the Last Dragon**, Scuttle in **The Little Mermaid**, Chump in **Migration**, and Zhen in **Kung Fu Panda 4**, and every one of them is her regular voice with zero attempt at differentiation. Give the role to someone who actually trained for voice work, someone who can disappear into a character instead of showing up as themselves with a different name on the poster.

## Also, I'm finally watching the MCU shows

I'm putting this in writing so I actually follow through. I've avoided most of the MCU shows for years. The only ones I've watched are **WandaVision**, **She-Hulk**, and more recently **Wonder Man**. But something's shifted lately, and I'm in the mood for superhero content, so I'm finally going to work through the ones I skipped.

No strategy yet on where to start. I just know the mood has hit and I'm not going to fight it this time.

**Consider this my public commitment.**
