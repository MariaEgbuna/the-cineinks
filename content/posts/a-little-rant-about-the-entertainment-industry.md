I have a few things sitting on my chest about television and film right now, and I'd rather dump them here than scream into the void on social media. This isn't a blind rage post. These are trends that have been quietly eating at me for years.

---

## Why does one season take three years?

*Severance* is the show that broke my spirit on this. Season 1 ended on its cliffhanger in April 2022, and Season 2 didn't arrive until January 2025. That is nearly three years for ten episodes of a mystery that relies on momentum. I know the 2023 Hollywood strikes scrambled production everywhere, but a three-year gap is becoming the baseline, and it's killing the medium.

*Blue Eye Samurai* is even worse. Season 1 dropped to huge acclaim in November 2023, and Season 2 isn't slated for Netflix until sometime in 2027. The creators have said the animation takes an immense amount of time, and I respect that. I want things to be good. But at some point the wait works against the art. The hype dies, people move on, and you end up re-watching Season 1 just to remember why you cared.

## Where did the lighting go, and why can't I hear anyone?

When did it become standard for whole scenes to be shot in pitch-black darkness? I like mood lighting and I like a good shadow. But there is a big difference between intentional, moody cinematography and not lighting the set properly. Too many modern shows use digital darkness as a shortcut for "atmosphere," and on a normal TV it just looks murky. Classic Hollywood built tension and noir without making us squint at two silhouettes and guess who is who.

The audio has the same problem. Dialogue is muffled, buried under bass, or drowned out by the score during action scenes. I shouldn't need a soundbar and an audio engineering degree to follow a conversation in a multi-million dollar production. Pitch-black visuals plus unclear audio doesn't look like prestige. It looks unpolished.

## Stop subsidizing the celebrity voice-acting grift

Leave it to the trained voice actors. I am saying this with my entire chest. Hollywood keeps casting A-list names in animated projects for the poster value, and most of them don't voice *characters*. They stand in a booth, read lines in their normal voice, and collect a big check. It breaks the illusion.

There are exceptions, and J.K. Simmons gets a lifetime pass. Yes, he sounds like J.K. Simmons as Omni-Man in *Invincible*, but that voice carries the weight and menace the character needs. He also has range: Kai in *Kung Fu Panda 3*, Mayor Lionheart in *Zootopia*, and Klaus don't sound like the same guy. He bends his performance to fit the animation.

On the other side, Awkwafina needs to be kept out of the recording booth. I am tired of seeing her name on animated projects. She has voiced Sisu in *Raya and the Last Dragon*, Scuttle in *The Little Mermaid*, Chump in *Migration*, and Zhen in *Kung Fu Panda 4*, and every one of those is just her regular speaking voice with no attempt at characterization. Give those roles back to the people who train for voice work and actually disappear into a character.

## A public accountability check: the MCU backlog

To end on a more positive note, I'm putting this in writing so I have to follow through: I'm finally tackling the MCU streaming backlog.

I avoided most of the Disney+ MCU shows for years. The only exceptions were *WandaVision*, *She-Hulk*, and the recent *Wonder Man*. But the craving for pure comic-book superhero content has hit me again, and I'm not going to fight it.

I don't have a plan for whether I'm going chronologically or just picking the coolest thumbnail first. If I drop off the face of the earth, assume I'm buried somewhere under Phase Four.
