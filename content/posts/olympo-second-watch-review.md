Streaming on Netflix, this Spanish drama was created by Jan Matheu, Laia Foguet, and Ibai Abad, and directed by Marçal Forès, Daniel Barone, Ibai Abad, and Ana Vázquez. The series stars Clara Galle, Agustín Della Corte, Nira Osahia, Nuno Gallego, María Romanillos, and Juan Perales.

*Spoiler note: This review covers the general premise without major plot twists. Content warning for substance abuse, body image pressure, and mature sexual themes.*

## The Premise

Set at the exclusive Pirineos High Performance Center in Spain, *Olympo* follows the country's top young athletic prospects as they train under immense physical and psychological pressure. When a teammate collapses under suspicious circumstances, synchronized swimming captain Amaia begins noticing sudden, unnatural spikes in her peers' athletic performances.

What starts as fierce competition for lucrative global sponsorships quickly spirals into a darker investigation. Determined to uncover how far the athletes and the institution are willing to go to win, Amaia starts digging into a web of performance-enhancing drugs, blackmail, and hidden alliances inside the training facility.

## What worked

![Clara Galle as Amaia and Agustín Della Corte as Roque](/images/posts/olympo-second-watch-review/amaia-roque.jpg)

The central premise is genuinely addictive, and the strongest parts of the show come down to its lead performances. Clara Galle is brilliant as Amaia, grounding the series with an intensity that sells her paranoia and ambition even when the script asks her to run in circles.

Right alongside her, Agustín Della Corte delivers a standout performance as rugby captain Roque Pérez. He brings real emotional weight to every scene, and his dynamic with Sebas (Juan Perales) easily produced my favorite moments of the season. Watching Roque and Sebas navigate hidden feelings, identity struggles, and locker-room pressure felt far more authentic and compelling than half of the main thriller plot.

## What didn't work

![Nira Osahia as Zoe and Renata lining up on the track](/images/posts/olympo-second-watch-review/what-did-not.jpg)

The biggest issue exposed on my second watch is the repetitive writing. Almost every episode falls into the exact same loop: Amaia senses something is off, investigates, hits a wall, and repeats the process all over again in the next episode. Several intriguing supporting athletes at the center are also left underdeveloped, making the world feel thinner than it should.

For a sports drama, the actual athletic sequences are also a total miss. The running scenes, in particular, look painfully average. You can visibly tell the actors aren't trained track athletes, which breaks the immersion whenever a race is supposed to feel elite. Using body doubles or tighter editing would have helped tremendously. On a smaller note, Nira Osahia as heptathlete Zoe never fully convinced me; she is passable, but her emotional beats felt flat next to stronger scene partners like Galle and Della Corte.

## The Netflix cancellation problem

Normally, a rough first season leaves room to hope the writers tighten the pacing and hire athletic doubles for Season 2. Here, there is no point. In classic Netflix fashion, *Olympo* was cancelled after a single season. What remains is a frustratingly unfinished story whose flaws feel permanent because the show never got the chance to fix them.

---

Watch it if you enjoy high-stakes teen mysteries or want a quick binge for the character dynamics alone.

**Fun fact:** Agustín Della Corte is a former World Cup rugby player in real life, which explains why he's practically the only cast member who actually looks like a pro athlete on screen.
