I put off *Silicon Valley* for years, assuming it was a niche comedy for people who write code. Then I watched all six seasons and could not believe I'd waited this long.

It looks like a joke about startups, but it's doing something much sharper underneath: showing very smart people pour themselves into pointless or harmful goals while believing they're changing the world. It's earned a Hall of Fame spot.

*This post has spoilers for HBO's Silicon Valley.*

## The Premise

Richard Hendricks is a programmer at a massive tech company called **Hooli** who builds a revolutionary data compression algorithm in his spare time. His boss tries to buy it for 10 million dollars, but Richard turns it down, takes a smaller investment, and starts his own company, **Pied Piper**. What follows is six seasons of everything that can go wrong for a company with a great product and a team of socially awkward people running it.

## The Characters

![Richard Hendricks in Silicon Valley](/images/posts/go-watch-silicon-valley-already/image-2.jpg)

**Richard Hendricks:** Thomas Middleditch plays a man who is brilliant at exactly one thing and useless at everything else. He panics constantly, second-guesses every decision, and can't handle confrontation. The tabs versus spaces breakup says it all: he dumps the only girl who has shown interest in him over a coding formatting preference.

![Erlich Bachman in Silicon Valley](/images/posts/go-watch-silicon-valley-already/image-3.jpg)

**Erlich Bachman:** The one character I could never get on board with. T.J. Miller plays the loud, self-obsessed owner of the incubator house, convinced of his own genius and inserting himself into every decision with way more confidence than usefulness. I enjoyed the show a lot more after he left at the end of Season 4.

![Gilfoyle and Dinesh in Silicon Valley](/images/posts/go-watch-silicon-valley-already/image-4.jpg)

**Bertram Gilfoyle:** The best character on the show, and I'm not taking questions on this one. Martin Starr plays him completely deadpan, unbothered by whatever disaster is unfolding, and somehow funnier than everyone else in the room. Against Dinesh (Kumail Nanjiani), who knows he's not as talented as the people around him and is desperate to prove himself, he's even better, and every loss Dinesh takes is funnier than it has any right to be.

![Jared Dunn in Silicon Valley](/images/posts/go-watch-silicon-valley-already/image-5.jpg)

**Donald "Jared" Dunn:** The strangest and most lovable character, no contest. Zach Woods plays a man who was clearly hurt in ways the show never fully explains, but keeps hinting at through casual mentions of disturbing stuff from his past. He is also the most loyal person on the team, and most of the show's emotional moments work because of him.

## Where did the jokes come from?

Creator Mike Judge came at it as someone who lived inside this culture and found it ridiculous. He worked at a Silicon Valley startup in 1987 and quit after three months, calling it a cult full of "true believers in something" who couldn't explain what that something was.

The writers also backed it up with real research, talking to engineers from Facebook, Google, Amazon, and Dropbox. The tabs versus spaces debate came straight from a dinner with real programmers. One writer texted an engineer afterward to check if the debate was real, and the reply confirmed it was, and that bringing it up had already started a screaming match at the bar.

## My One Complaint

Seasons 3 and 4 fall into the same loop too often. Pied Piper nearly dies, something saves it at the last second, they almost pivot into something totally different, that nearly collapses too, and repeat. Once you spot the pattern it gets predictable, but the cast carries it so well that the details of each disaster barely matter.

## Is Silicon Valley Hall of Fame material?

Yes. It's sharp, funny, and smarter about the industry it mocks than people give it credit for. The show is stressful in the best way: you keep watching not because things are going well, but because you need to see how they claw their way out of the latest mess. 

By all means the show isn't perfect, but the cast is one of the best ensembles HBO has ever put together, the finale works, and it gets more relevant every year.

Hall of Fame. No question about it.
