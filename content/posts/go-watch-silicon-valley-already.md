I finished Silicon Valley recently, and I honestly cannot believe it took me this long to watch it.

Six seasons, 53 episodes, and it held up almost the whole way through. Silicon Valley looks like a niche comedy on the surface, but it is doing something way sharper underneath, and it deserves a proper Hall of Fame spot. So here we are.

_This post has minor spoilers for HBO's Silicon Valley, heads up._

* * *

## The plot, in a nutshell

Richard Hendricks is a programmer at a massive tech company called Hooli who builds a revolutionary data compression algorithm in his spare time. His boss tries to buy it off him for 10 million dollars, but Richard turns it down, takes a smaller investment instead, and starts his own company called Pied Piper. What follows is six seasons of everything that can possibly go wrong for a company with a great product and a team of socially awkward people trying to run it.

* * *

## My Take

Silicon Valley is fun from start to finish. It's stressful in the best way, the kind of show where you keep watching not because things are going well, but because you need to see how they claw their way out of the latest mess. The comedy is sharp without trying too hard.

My only complaint is that Seasons 3 and 4 fall into the same loop too often: Pied Piper nearly dies, something saves it at the last second, they almost pivot into something totally different, that nearly collapses too, and repeat. Once you spot the pattern it gets predictable, but the cast carries it so well that the details of each disaster barely matter. And the finale, where the team destroys their own creation because the AI they built could cause serious global damage, hits a lot differently in 2026 than it did when it aired in December 2019.

## The Characters

### RICHARD HENDRICKS

![Go Watch Silicon Valley Already: HBO's Tech Comedy Is Hall of Fame Material](/images/posts/go-watch-silicon-valley-already/image-2.jpg)

Thomas Middleditch plays a man who's brilliant at exactly one thing and pretty much useless at everything else. He panics constantly, second guesses every decision, and can't handle confrontation. The tabs versus spaces breakup says it all: he dumps the only girl who's ever shown interest in him over a coding formatting preference. Frustrating and hilarious at the same time.

### ERLICH BACHMAN

![Go Watch Silicon Valley Already: HBO's Tech Comedy Is Hall of Fame Material](/images/posts/go-watch-silicon-valley-already/image-3.jpg)

The one character I could never get on board with. T.J. Miller plays the loud, self-obsessed owner of the incubator house, convinced of his own genius at all times and inserting himself into every decision with way more confidence than usefulness. I enjoyed the show a lot more after he left at the end of Season 4.

### BERTRAM GILFOYLE

![Go Watch Silicon Valley Already: HBO's Tech Comedy Is Hall of Fame Material](/images/posts/go-watch-silicon-valley-already/image-4.jpg)

The best character on the show, and I'm not taking questions on this one. Martin Starr plays him completely deadpan, unbothered by whatever disaster is unfolding, and somehow funnier than everyone else in the room without trying. He's the most dangerous person in any low stakes situation, and it never stops being funny.

### DONALD "JARED" DUNN

![Go Watch Silicon Valley Already: HBO's Tech Comedy Is Hall of Fame Material](/images/posts/go-watch-silicon-valley-already/image-5.jpg)

The strangest and most lovable character, no contest. Zach Woods plays a man who was clearly hurt in ways the show never fully explains, but keeps hinting at through sudden, casual mentions of disturbing stuff from his past. He's also the most loyal person on the team, and most of the show's emotional moments work because of him. He's the one who cares about the people around him, not just the product.

### DINESH CHUGTAI

![Go Watch Silicon Valley Already: HBO's Tech Comedy Is Hall of Fame Material](/images/posts/go-watch-silicon-valley-already/image-6.jpg)

Good enough to know he's not as talented as the people around him, which makes him constantly desperate to prove himself in ways that always backfire. Against Gilfoyle he's great. Kumail Nanjiani plays wounded pride at full volume, and every loss Dinesh takes is funnier than it has any right to be.

## Why it works

The reason Silicon Valley works so well is that Mike Judge (Creator) came at it from the perspective of someone who actually lived inside this culture and found it ridiculous. He worked at a Silicon Valley startup in 1987 and quit after three months, calling it a cult full of people who were "true believers in something" without being able to explain what that something even was. The frustration when watching extremely smart people pour themselves into goals that are either pointless or actively harmful while believing they are changing the world, is what powers every single episode.

The writers also backed all of it up with real research, talking to actual engineers from Facebook, Google, Amazon, and Dropbox to make sure the details held up. The tabs versus spaces debate, for example, came straight out of a dinner the writers had with real programmers. One writer even texted an engineer afterward just to check if the debate was real, and got back a message confirming it was, and that simply bringing it up had already started a screaming match at the bar. They put it in the show almost exactly as it happened. That level of attention to detail is what separates Silicon Valley from other tech comedies that just settle for easy jokes about hoodies and ping pong tables.

## Final Verdict

Silicon Valley is sharp, funny, and smarter about the industry it is making fun of than people usually give it credit for. Sure it's not perfect: the middle seasons get a bit repetitive, and sometimes Erlich's a lot to handle. But the cast is one of the best ensembles HBO has ever put together, the finale sticks the landing, and it somehow gets more relevant with every year that passes. Hall of Fame. No question about it.
