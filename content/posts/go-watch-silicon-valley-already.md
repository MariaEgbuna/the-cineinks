I finished Silicon Valley recently, and I honestly cannot believe it took me this long to watch it.

Six seasons, 53 episodes, and it held up almost the whole way through. Silicon Valley looks like a niche comedy on the surface, but it is doing something way sharper underneath, and it deserves a proper Hall of Fame spot. So here we are.

_This post has minor spoilers for HBO's Silicon Valley, heads up._

* * *

### The plot, in a nutshell

Richard Hendricks is a programmer at a massive tech company called Hooli who builds a revolutionary data compression algorithm in his spare time. His boss tries to buy it off him for 10 million dollars, but Richard turns it down, takes a smaller investment instead, and starts his own company called Pied Piper. What follows is six seasons of everything that can possibly go wrong for a company with a great product and a team of socially awkward people trying to run it.

* * *

### My Take

Silicon Valley is fun from start to finish. It is stressful in the best way possible, the kind of show where you keep watching not because things are going well, but because you desperately need to see how they claw their way out of whatever mess they just made. The comedy is sharp without trying too hard, and the show is smarter about the tech industry than it looks on the surface.

My only complaint is that Seasons 3 and 4 fall into the same loop a bit too often: Pied Piper nearly dies, something saves it at the last second, they almost pivot into something totally different, that nearly collapses too, and Repeat. Once you spot the pattern it gets predictable. But the cast carries it so well that the details of each new disaster barely matter, because you are watching for the characters. And the finale, where the team destroys their own creation because the AI they built could end up causing serious global damage, hits a lot differently in 2026 than it probably did back when it aired in December 2019.

### The Characters

#### RICHARD HENDRICKS:

![Go Watch Silicon Valley Already](/images/posts/go-watch-silicon-valley-already/image-2.jpg)

Richard is brilliant at exactly one thing and pretty much useless at everything else. He panics constantly, second guesses every decision, and cannot handle confrontation without either freezing up or blowing it out of proportion. The tabs versus spaces breakup basically sums him up completely. He dumps the only girl who has ever shown interest in him over a coding formatting preference that changes literally nothing about how the software works. A genius who will torch his own life over a point of principle that only he cares about. It's frustrating and hilarious at the same time.

#### ERLICH BACHMAN:

![Go Watch Silicon Valley Already](/images/posts/go-watch-silicon-valley-already/image-3.jpg)

He's the one character I could never fully get on board with. Played by T.J. Miller, his character is loud, self obsessed, and convinced of his own genius at all times. He owns the incubator house where most of the team lives, and inserts himself into every decision with way more confidence than usefulness. There are moments where there's a flash of insight buried under all that noise, but he was just too much for me to handle consistently. I actually enjoyed the show a lot more after he left at the end of Season 4.

#### BERTRAM GILFOYLE:

![Go Watch Silicon Valley Already](/images/posts/go-watch-silicon-valley-already/image-4.jpg)

The best character on the show, and I am not taking questions on this one. Martin Starr plays him completely deadpan, totally unbothered by whatever disaster is unfolding around him, and somehow funnier than everyone else in the room without even trying. Gilfoyle is the most dangerous person in any low stakes situation, and it never stops being funny.

#### DONALD "JARED" DUNN

![Go Watch Silicon Valley Already](/images/posts/go-watch-silicon-valley-already/image-5.jpg)

The strangest and most lovable character on the show, no contest. This man was clearly hurt in ways the show never fully explains, but keeps hinting at through these sudden, casual mentions of genuinely disturbing stuff from his past. Jared is also by far the most loyal person on the Pied Piper team, and most of the show's emotional moments land because of him, since he is the one character who actually cares about the people around him and not just the product.

#### DINESH CHUGTAI:

![Go Watch Silicon Valley Already](/images/posts/go-watch-silicon-valley-already/image-6.webp)

He is someone who is good enough to know he is not quite as talented as the people around him, which makes him constantly desperate to prove himself in ways that always backfire. On his own he is fine. Against Gilfoyle he is great. Watching the two of them go back and forth in petty one-upmanship is where Dinesh really shines, and Nanjiani's ability to play wounded pride at full volume makes every loss he takes funnier than it has any right to be.

### Honorable Mentions:

#### NELSON "BIG HEAD" BIGHETTI:

![Go Watch Silicon Valley Already](/images/posts/go-watch-silicon-valley-already/image-7.jpg)

Big Head is not very good at his job, has no real direction, and barely seems to know what his job even is at any given moment. By the finale he somehow ends up President of Stanford University. The joke is not just that he is bad at his job, it is that the tech industry sometimes reward people who happen to be in the right place at the right time, no matter what they actually contribute. There is even a real term for this, _rest and vest_, and Big Head might be the most accurate portrait of it TV has ever made.

#### GAVIN BELSON:

![Go Watch Silicon Valley Already](/images/posts/go-watch-silicon-valley-already/image-8.jpeg)

Hooli's CEO and he is basically a walking portrait of a tech billionaire who has been told he is a genius for so long that he has completely lost touch with reality. He's surrounded by yes-people who enable his worst ideas every step of the way, burning huge amounts of money on vanity projects and making decisions based on his ego before his actual business. He is a joke, but a very specific kind of joke that anyone who has ever worked inside a big company will recognize instantly.

### Why it works

The reason Silicon Valley works so well is that Mike Judge (Creator) came at it from the perspective of someone who actually lived inside this culture and found it ridiculous. He worked at a Silicon Valley startup in 1987 and quit after three months, calling it a cult full of people who were "true believers in something" without being able to explain what that something even was. The frustration when watching extremely smart people pour themselves into goals that are either pointless or actively harmful while believing they are changing the world, is what powers every single episode.

The writers also backed all of it up with real research, talking to actual engineers from Facebook, Google, Amazon, and Dropbox to make sure the details held up. The tabs versus spaces debate, for example, came straight out of a dinner the writers had with real programmers. One writer even texted an engineer afterward just to check if the debate was real, and got back a message confirming it was, and that simply bringing it up had already started a screaming match at the bar. They put it in the show almost exactly as it happened. That level of attention to detail is what separates Silicon Valley from other tech comedies that just settle for easy jokes about hoodies and ping pong tables.

### Final Verdict

Silicon Valley is sharp, funny, and smarter about the industry it is making fun of than people usually give it credit for. Sure it's not perfect: the middle seasons get a bit repetitive, and sometimes Erlich's a lot to handle. But the cast is one of the best ensembles HBO has ever put together, the finale sticks the landing, and it somehow gets more relevant with every year that passes. Hall of Fame. No question about it.
