I posted [how big things break my brain](https://thewatchlistchronicles.blogspot.com/2026/05/why-big-things-break-my-brain-and-im.html) on the blog, then someone asked; "What about Transformers?"

In all these years, I'd only ever watched the first movie from 2007. So I sat down and watched all five Michael Bay films back to back, to see whether the giant robot thing would finally click the way the MonsterVerse did. It didn't, but the marathon wasn't a waste either.

*This post contains minor spoilers for the Transformers movies.*

---

## The first one still works: Transformers 2007

![Michael Bay Hurt My Brain: A Transformers Breakdown](/images/posts/michael-bay-hurt-my-brain-a-transformers-breakdown/image-2.jpg)

**Transformers (2007)** takes the crown, no argument. The story makes sense from start to finish, which sounds like a low bar until you watch the other four.

Shia LaBeouf is why it holds together. Sam isn't some chiseled action hero; he's sweating, panicking, and confused as hell. And it works. If somebody told me my car was a 15 foot alien robot, I'd be shouting "what do you mean my car is alive" the whole movie too. He plays a normal person reacting like a normal person – the other films lose that almost completely. Megan Fox as Mikaela was fine. She's the love interest who can also fix engines, and that's all the story needed. The Mission City battle still holds up, with robots that actually interact with the environment instead of just smashing through it.

It made over 700 million dollars worldwide on a 150 million dollar budget, so I wasn't the only one who thought it worked.

**Plot: 7.6. CGI: 9.** The one Transformers movie I'd rewatch without rolling my eyes.

## Where the wheels came off

![Michael Bay Hurt My Brain: A Transformers Breakdown](/images/posts/michael-bay-hurt-my-brain-a-transformers-breakdown/image-3.jpg)

**Revenge of the Fallen (2009)** is weak. Too many threads pulling in too many directions, too many characters fighting for screen time, and even the action gets buried under the noise. Then there's Skids and Mudflap, who talk in exaggerated slang, can't read, and one of them has a gold tooth. The comic relief is lazy and a little embarrassing. It still made over 836 million dollars on a 200 million dollar budget. Numbers don't tell you about quality. They just tell you people trusted the brand enough to show up.

**Plot: 4. CGI: 8.7.** The robots still look good even when the story doesn't.

![Michael Bay Hurt My Brain: A Transformers Breakdown](/images/posts/michael-bay-hurt-my-brain-a-transformers-breakdown/image-4.jpg)

**Dark of the Moon (2011)** took me three days to finish. Not because it's long, though it is, but because my brain kept tapping out halfway through.

Megan Fox is gone here. In a 2009 interview with Wonderland magazine, she compared Bay to Hitler. Bay later told GQ that Spielberg, the executive producer, told him to fire her over it. Spielberg has denied that, and Fox's side says she chose to leave. Either way, Rosie Huntington-Whiteley took over as the love interest. The new girl can't act well, though she isn't given much to work with.

The story is a step up from Revenge of the Fallen, with a clearer through line about a hidden Cybertronian ship on the moon. But the action asks you to turn your brain off completely. These humans survive falling buildings and point blank explosions and just walk it off, and at some point I started laughing at the movie. It's also the highest grossing film in the series at over 1.1 billion dollars, and the CGI holds up as always.

**Plot: 5.9. CGI: 8.8.**

## The Ones With Mark Wahlberg

![Michael Bay Hurt My Brain: A Transformers Breakdown](/images/posts/michael-bay-hurt-my-brain-a-transformers-breakdown/image-5.jpg)

**Age of Extinction (2014)** is a total reset on the human side: new lead, new daughter, new villain. Mark Wahlberg doesn't work for me. He's performing confidence instead of carrying it.

The bigger problem is Tessa and her secret boyfriend Shane. When Wahlberg's character threatens to call the police, Shane pulls the "Romeo and Juliet law" out of his wallet. Tessa is 17 and Shane is 20. It's played as a joke, and I found it deeply uncomfortable. Jack Reynor, who played Shane, told The Hollywood Reporter that, especially since the Me Too movement, it's "definitely a problematic joke," even though he didn't write it.

By this point I'd stopped looking for logic in the bigger plot. Why is a tech company building Transformers from scratch? Why are the Dinobots suddenly a thing, dinosaur robots locked up by a bounty hunter from space? None of it connects to what came before. It just exists because the franchise needed something new to sell. The story does feel more deliberate than Dark of the Moon, and it crossed 1.1 billion dollars too, so my complaints clearly didn't matter to the box office.

**Plot: 6.4. CGI: 9.**

![Michael Bay Hurt My Brain: A Transformers Breakdown](/images/posts/michael-bay-hurt-my-brain-a-transformers-breakdown/image-6.jpg)

**The Last Knight (2017)** feels like the team did whatever they wanted. King Arthur, Merlin, ancient Transformer knights, and a Cybertronian goddess who brainwashes Optimus Prime into a villain. I watched it days ago and I'm already struggling to remember the plot, which tells you something. The CGI is excellent, some of the best in the series. This is also where the box office finally cracked. It had the lowest opening of the five and finished around 600 million dollars worldwide, well below Age of Extinction's billion plus.

**Plot: 5.8. CGI: 8.9.**

## The action problem nobody talks about enough

My real issue across all five films isn't the plot. Bay's editing, his lighting, and the overcomplicated robot designs make the action hard to follow. The robots have so many moving panels and overlapping pieces of metal that in a fight you lose track of which limb belongs to who. Add the fast cuts and the tight, chaotic framing, and the big battles turn into a blur of metal and sparks. It's less about whether you can see the screen and more about whether your brain can process it fast enough.

The first film handles this slightly better because the pacing gives you more room. The sequels speed everything up and throw more robots into frame.

---

## Did this make me a Transformers person?

No. Not the way the MonsterVerse did, and not the way Attack on Titan did. I don't regret the marathon, but I never got fully invested.

Part of it is scale. I like Optimus Prime, I like Bumblebee, and there's a real charm to these robots. But they don't feel big in a "_this thing could swallow a city block_" way, and I want Pacific Rim kaiju scale or Attack on Titan colossal scale. That matters more to me than I expected going in.

The first film holds up. The rest range from fine to forgettable, with Revenge of the Fallen as the clear low point.

**My brain survived all five, but the giant robot thing still didn't click.**
