I already wrote about how [gay romance in media has a suffering problem](https://cineinks.vercel.app/posts/gay-romance-in-media-has-a-suffering-problem) a few months back, and then I went and watched Free Fall anyway, fully aware I was walking into exactly the kind of story I was complaining about.

Free Fall, or Freier Fall in its original German title, is a 2013 drama directed by Stephan Lacant, who co-wrote it with Karsten Dahlem. It premiered at the Berlin Film Festival in February that year, and it got enough international attention that people started comparing it to Brokeback Mountain – a film I've never watched and doubt I ever will. The comparison still tells you what kind of story you're in for.

*This post contains spoilers for Free Fall (2013).*

---

## What it's about

Marc Borgmann (Hanno Koffler) is a young police officer training for a riot control unit, living with his pregnant girlfriend Bettina (Katharina Schüttler). At the academy he meets his new roommate, Kay Engel (Max Riemelt). They don't get along at first, but they become friends, and it shifts once they start running together outside training. Kay makes the first move, kissing Marc during a jog. Marc pulls away, but not for long, and the two start sharing intimate moments in secret, all while Marc renovates the house he and Bettina are moving into before the baby arrives.

From there, the story is less about the relationship and more about the fallout of Marc living two separate lives. It's a slow, uncomfortable unraveling instead of a big dramatic reveal, which makes it hit harder.

## The characters

![REVIEW: Free Fall (2013)](/images/posts/free-fall-wrecked-me-and-i-kind-of-expected-it-to/bett-and-marc.jpg)

Marc is who the film spends its time with, and I found him pretty self-centered for most of the runtime. But I get where it comes from. He's terrified of his own feelings, and more terrified of what accepting them will cost: his relationship, his career, his standing with people who have casual homophobia baked into their everyday conversations. He doesn't come clean until he has no other choice, and he lets the people closest to him absorb the damage of decisions he's too scared to make out loud. It's not likable, but it's believable, and those aren't the same thing.

Bettina is the real victim of this story. She's pregnant, building a life with a man who's deceiving her, and she finds out she's been cheated on while carrying his child. She isn't written as an obstacle between Marc and the "real" love story. She's a person wronged by someone she trusted, and every bit of suspicion or frustration she shows makes sense.

![REVIEW: Free Fall (2013)](/images/posts/free-fall-wrecked-me-and-i-kind-of-expected-it-to/kay.jpg)

Kay never gets the same depth. I went in because I liked Max Riemelt in Sense8 – a show that got cancelled way before it should have – and by the end I still didn't feel like I knew Kay as a person. The film keeps him at arm's length, more a catalyst for Marc's unraveling than a fully realized character. That's my biggest disappointment with the movie.

## Why the sad ending didn't bother me

There was a trend in the 2010s of making queer stories synonymous with tragedy, like happiness was off the table by default. Free Fall does end sadly, and Marc loses basically everything. But it didn't bother me the way those usually do, because the sadness was the point from the start, not a twist thrown in to punish the audience for getting invested.

You know the type. Everything's going fine, the couple's happy, and then out of nowhere the movie pulls a death by hate crime, drops a cheating subplot out of left field, or introduces an AIDS diagnosis in the final act like a narrative middle finger to anyone who hoped. Free Fall doesn't do that. Marc has to hit rock bottom – exactly what the title points toward the whole time – before he can accept who he is and start building something honest. It's a sad ending, but a hopeful one.

## The sequel that may never happen

I've heard rumors of a sequel for years. There was a crowdfunding campaign back in 2017 to get Free Fall 2 made, with the same core cast attached, but it's still sitting in "in development" limbo with no plot details public. I'm curious where they'd take Marc after this, but I'm not holding my breath.

---

## Final thoughts

All in all, it was a decent flick – well-acted, quietly devastating, and I don't regret watching it. I don't think it deserves to be lumped in with the more cynical, punishment-driven tragedies that plagued this genre that decade.

I need a serious pick-me-up after this one. I'm tired of sad gay movies, and I think I deserve a break before I go looking for the next one.
