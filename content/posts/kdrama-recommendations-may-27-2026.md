I did not plan to become a K-drama person. It happened quietly, the way most obsessions do.

One show, then another, and suddenly you're eight episodes deep with no intention of stopping. The K-dramas that stick with me are rarely the ones making the most noise. The massive titles get recommended endlessly, and some of them deserve it, but there's a tier of shows just below the mainstream conversation doing things just as interesting. This list is mostly those, with one deliberate exception I'll get to. They're not ranked. They're just the shows that took a permanent spot in my memory.

---

### Evilive (2023)

![K-Drama Recommendations: 9 Shows That Stuck With Me](/images/posts/kdrama-recommendations-may-27-2026/evilive.jpg)

**2023 · 1 Season · 10 Episodes · Completed**

This is the show where Kim Young-Kwang broke my brain. I'd seen him in lighter roles, the kind of romantic lead you enjoy and quickly forget. Evilive is where I realized what he's capable of. He plays Seo Do-Young, a gangster who's calculating, cold, and in complete control, without ever tipping into cartoonish villainy. It's a noir-soaked crime thriller about a disgraced lawyer pulled into online gambling who slowly loses himself to it. You watch the lawyer become monstrous while the gangster becomes someone you reluctantly understand. Since watching this, I've tracked down nearly everything Kim Young-Kwang has been in.

**What could have been better:** The pacing drags in the middle, and the plot sometimes leans on coincidence. The performances carry you through it.

---

### Strangers from Hell (2019)

![K-Drama Recommendations: 9 Shows That Stuck With Me](/images/posts/kdrama-recommendations-may-27-2026/strangers-from-hell.jpg)

**2019 · 1 Season · 10 Episodes · Completed**

"Eerie" doesn't begin to cover it. This show is unsettling in a way that's hard to describe without spoiling it, and the atmosphere does the heavy lifting before anything explicit happens. A young man moves into a dirt-cheap apartment building in Seoul, and his neighbors are just wrong. The dread builds slowly, and by the time the show delivers on what it's been hinting at, you're too deep to look away. Im Siwan and Lee Dong-Wook turn in some of the best work of their careers, moving in opposite directions: one unraveling, the other disturbingly composed.

**What could have been better:** Ten episodes feels short, and some of the psychological groundwork needed more room. The ending divides people, and I get why.

---

### A Killer Paradox (2024)

![K-Drama Recommendations: 9 Shows That Stuck With Me](/images/posts/kdrama-recommendations-may-27-2026/image-4.jpg)

**2024 · 1 Season · 8 Episodes · Completed**

A broke, directionless college student accidentally kills a man in self-defense, only to find out he was a serial killer. Then it happens again. The show doesn't rush you toward an easy moral conclusion. It holds the question open the whole time: is he doing something wrong, or accidentally doing something right? Choi Woo-Shik plays a bewildered, guilt-ridden spiral better than anyone in Korean drama right now, and Son Suk-Ku brings a world-weary energy to the detective hunting him. Every scene they share feels like a slow-motion collision.

**What could have been better:** A few supporting threads are left dangling in a way that feels less like ambiguity and more like the writers ran out of runway.

### The Kidnapping Day (2023)

![K-Drama Recommendations: 9 Shows That Stuck With Me](/images/posts/kdrama-recommendations-may-27-2026/image-5.jpg)

**2023 · 1 Season · 12 Episodes · Completed**

Nobody talks about this show, and it baffles me. It's a black comedy thriller about a clumsy, desperate dad who kidnaps an eleven-year-old genius girl to pay for his own daughter's medical bills. The twist is that the girl is way sharper than he is, and everything spirals. It's funny in a way K-dramas rarely allow: darkly, uncomfortably funny, where you catch yourself laughing at things you probably shouldn't. It's the most purely entertaining thing I watched all year.

**What could have been better:** The tonal shifts aren't always seamless. It sometimes sits awkwardly between tense and comedic instead of nailing either.

---

### Moving (2023)

![K-Drama Recommendations: 9 Shows That Stuck With Me](/images/posts/kdrama-recommendations-may-27-2026/moving.jpg)

**2023 · 1 Season · 20 Episodes · Completed**

Moving is the best thing the superhero genre has produced in years, and I say that as someone who's watched the genre exhaust itself on joyless Hollywood spectacles. What this show understands is that the power is never the point. The people are. The adults hide their abilities to protect their kids, while the teenagers discover their own powers with no idea their parents know how terrifying that shift is. That generational tension runs for twenty episodes without losing steam, and the final stretch is some of the best television I've ever seen. Not best K-drama. Best television. Full stop.

**What could have been better:** The first few episodes are a deliberate slow-burn, and some viewers tap out early. If you're three episodes in and feel like nothing's happening, trust the process. A few of the villain backstory episodes also run long.

### Flower of Evil (2020)

![K-Drama Recommendations: 9 Shows That Stuck With Me](/images/posts/kdrama-recommendations-may-27-2026/image-7.jpg)

**2020 · 1 Season · 16 Episodes · Completed**

A detective slowly realizes her husband isn't who she thought he was. Lee Joon-Gi plays a man who's suppressed his true identity so completely that he's become a near-perfect copy of a loving husband. The performance is precise and unsettling, because you're watching someone work hard to mimic feelings they don't have, until something real slips through. The chemistry between the two leads is extraordinary, one of the best screen pairs in recent memory.

**What could have been better:** The show leans on its central mystery a little too long, and a few back-half episodes are clearly marking time. A tighter edit would have made it flawless instead of just exceptional.

---

### The Worst of Evil (2023)

![K-Drama Recommendations: 9 Shows That Stuck With Me](/images/posts/kdrama-recommendations-may-27-2026/the-worst-of-evil.jpg)

**2023 · 1 Season · 12 Episodes · Completed**

An undercover cop drama about a detective who infiltrates a massive criminal organization running the drug trade between Korea, China, and Japan. Ji Chang-Wook goes so deep undercover that the line between who he's pretending to be and who he's becoming starts to blur dangerously. Wi Ha-Joon is fantastic as his criminal counterpart, and every scene they share crackles with tension. It barely gets mentioned anywhere, which is a mystery to me.

**What could have been better:** The show loses its nerve in the final few episodes. The ending pulls toward a cleaner resolution than the story needed, and you can feel the compromise.

---

### Twinkling Watermelon (2023)

![K-Drama Recommendations: 9 Shows That Stuck With Me](/images/posts/kdrama-recommendations-may-27-2026/image-9.jpg)

**2023 · 1 Season · 16 Episodes · Completed**

This one caught me off guard, because it has no business being this emotionally precise. It's the tonal outlier on the list: a time-travel, coming-of-age story centered on music, warmer and stranger than you'd expect. A teenager accidentally travels back to 1995 and meets his parents when they were teenagers themselves, before they became the versions he grew up with. One minute it's a fun time-travel story, and then suddenly you're crying in episode fourteen, unsure when that happened.

**What could have been better:** The romance subplot runs longer than necessary, and a few middle episodes meander. The young cast more than makes up for it.

---

### The Glory (2022)

![K-Drama Recommendations: 9 Shows That Stuck With Me](/images/posts/kdrama-recommendations-may-27-2026/the-glory.jpg)

**2022 · 1 Season (2 Parts) · 16 Episodes · Completed**

This is my one deliberate exception, because The Glory is definitely not underrated. It was one of the most talked-about dramas of the last few years. I'm including it because I think the mainstream conversation flattened it into a standard revenge thriller, when it's doing something more precise. Song Hye-Kyo plays a woman who spent years constructing a life designed to ruin the people who brutally bullied her in high school. The drama is in the planning, not the execution, and watching her operate like a grandmaster is one of the most satisfying things I've seen. Part 1 alone is close to flawless. If you haven't watched it, close this tab and go fix that. I'll still be here.

**What could have been better:** Part 2 doesn't match the surgical precision of Part 1. The final episodes aren't bad, but you can feel the drop.

That's my list. I'll be adding to it in the future, but these are the shows that took a permanent spot in my memory.
