I keep a database of every movie and show I've watched since 2025, which sounds a bit much until a friend asks what's been good this year and I can answer in ten seconds.

So here's the real list, pulled straight from my own ratings: the ten movies I rated highest from January to June. Some are new, some are old rewatches, and the tenth spot landed at 7.7. Nothing is ranked by anything other than how hard it hooked me.

---

## 10. Dhurandhar (2025) - 7.7

![Dhurandhar](/images/posts/best-movies-ive-watched-this-year-so-far/image-2.jpg)

I saw a tweet about this one and figured I'd give it a shot. It's a Bollywood spy thriller from Aditya Dhar, starring Ranveer Singh as an undercover Indian intelligence agent who infiltrates Karachi's criminal underworld. Big, loud, ambitious, and not your typical Bollywood outing.

The ending caught me off guard because it just sort of stops. The story splits into two parts, and the sequel already dropped earlier this year (I haven't gotten to it yet). Fair warning: it runs over three and a half hours.

## 09. End of Watch (2012) - 7.9

![End of Watch](/images/posts/best-movies-ive-watched-this-year-so-far/image-3.jpg)

My brother put me onto this one. It follows two LAPD partners, played by Jake Gyllenhaal and Michael Pena, patrolling one of the roughest divisions in South Central LA. I'm not rushing back to rewatch it, but it did exactly what it set out to do.

## 08. The Gift (2015) - 8.3

![The Gift](/images/posts/best-movies-ive-watched-this-year-so-far/image-4.jpg)

I'd heard about this one for years and never got around to it. Watched it this year and holy shit. Jason Bateman and Rebecca Hall play a married couple who run into an old acquaintance from Bateman's character's past, played by Joel Edgerton.

What starts as an awkward reunion slowly turns into something much darker.

## 07. Marty Supreme (2025) - 8.6

![Marty Supreme](/images/posts/best-movies-ive-watched-this-year-so-far/image-5.jpg)

This one gave me Uncut Gems vibes immediately, and that's not a coincidence: it's directed by Josh Safdie, solo this time without his brother Benny. It's not on the same level, but the anxiety it creates is the same kind. Timothee Chalamet plays Marty Mauser, a hustler in 1950s New York obsessed with becoming a table tennis champion nobody respects.

There's so much happening on screen at once that my own anxiety spiked, and it took me two days to finish because I needed breaks. That's not a complaint by the way.

## 06. No Other Choice (2025) - 8.8

![No Other Choice](/images/posts/best-movies-ive-watched-this-year-so-far/image-6.jpg)

This one starts out as a soft workplace comedy. Lee Byung-hun plays a paper industry veteran laid off after 25 years who decides the only way to secure a new job is to eliminate the competition, literally. It's so fucking good. The runtime is a little long, but it's worth every minute once the back half kicks in.

## 05. Transformers One (2024) - 8.9

![Transformers One](/images/posts/best-movies-ive-watched-this-year-so-far/image-7.jpg)

This is the best Transformers movie ever made, and once you watch it, you'll see I'm not exaggerating. It's an animated prequel about the origin of Optimus Prime and Megatron, back when they were coworkers named Orion Pax and D-16 working in the mines of Cybertron.

The marketing was brutal. It was made to look like a kids' movie and so, tanked at the box office despite great reviews. I just hope we eventually get the sequel this story deserves.

## 04. Hoppers (2026) - 9.0

![Hoppers](/images/posts/best-movies-ive-watched-this-year-so-far/image-9.jpg)

I only watched this because of a TikTok edit, which feels like a very 2026 way to discover a Pixar movie. It follows Mabel, a college student who transfers her consciousness into a robotic beaver to communicate with animals and protect their habitat from a mayor's development plans.

I had an absolute blast. It gets chaotic about halfway through and never lets up, and the voice cast, especially Piper Curda and Bobby Moynihan, fully commits to the insanity of the premise.

## 03. John Wick (2014) - 9.2

![John Wick](/images/posts/best-movies-ive-watched-this-year-so-far/image-8.jpg)

This was a rewatch, and according to my database it's near the top of my ratings for the entire year so far. John Wick is so stylish and so confident in what it is. The action choreography still holds up over a decade later, and Keanu Reeves brings a level of physical commitment that elevates the whole thing.

I forgot how much he actually acts in this one. A simple revenge movie about a man and his dog kicked off one of the most beloved action franchises of the last decade, and watching it again reminds you why.

## 02. The Handmaiden (2016) - 9.3

![The Handmaiden](/images/posts/best-movies-ive-watched-this-year-so-far/image-11.jpg)

I'm a little late getting to this one, but I'm glad I finally did. It's told from multiple perspectives, and without spoiling anything, it's a queer love story that actually gets a good ending. That alone makes it worth the watch.

The way the story keeps recontextualizing itself is what makes it unforgettable, and it rewards a rewatch almost immediately, because you start noticing what was hiding in plain sight. I love this movie so much.

## 01. Predator: Killer of Killers (2025) - 9.4

![Predator: Killer of Killers](/images/posts/best-movies-ive-watched-this-year-so-far/image-10.jpg)

I'd never seen the original Predator movies. The first one I watched was Prey, and I liked it well enough, so I went into this animated anthology with low expectations. I came out with my mind blown.

It tells three stories across three eras, a Viking raid, a feudal Japan duel between brothers, and a WWII dogfight, all building toward something bigger. The art style is gorgeous: slick, brutal, and confident in a way animated action rarely is. I need more of this.
